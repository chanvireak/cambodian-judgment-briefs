This repository contains research notes. It is **not legal advice**, not a legal opinion, and not a
substitute for advice from a qualified Cambodian lawyer. Reading it, quoting it, or relying on it creates
no lawyer–client relationship with anyone, and no warranty of any kind.

If you have a legal problem, instruct counsel. If you are counsel, verify everything against the judgment.

## Machine-generated content

Every brief in `briefs/` was written by an AI agent (a large language model) reading the OCR text of a
Khmer-language judgment. The agent could not read the original PDF layout, could not ask the court what it
meant, and could not consult commentary. Consequences you should assume are present somewhere in this
corpus:

- **Misread facts.** OCR inserts spaces inside Khmer words, drops digits, and splices page boilerplate into
  sentences. A date or an amount can be wrong.
- **Misassigned roles.** Plaintiff, defendant, appellant and guarantor can be confused, especially where a
  judgment changes posture between instances.
- **Wrong weight.** A proposition may be described as the ratio when the court treated it as obiter, or the
  reverse. Only the judgment decides that.
- **Confident prose over thin text.** Where the source is damaged, a brief may read more smoothly than the
  judgment deserves. Section 6 of each brief is meant to disclose exactly these cases — read it.

## What the pin cites do and do not prove

Each brief cites `[Para N]`, where N is a line number in a machine-numbered version of the judgment text.
Every cite was checked to resolve to an existing line before the brief was accepted. That check proves the
cite *exists*; it does not prove the sentence supports the claim built on it. That second check is a human
task.

## No warranty of currency or completeness

The corpus is a snapshot of 108 judgments (2015–2020). Judgments can be appealed, set aside, or overtaken by
later law. Nothing here tracks those developments. The corpus covers civil matters only and only what
happened to be in one document library.

## Privacy

Parties, children and counsel appear as initials or `(name redacted)`. Judges are named as they are named on
the face of the judgment. If you believe a name should be removed, open an issue and it will be.

## Corrections

Corrections, counter-readings and methodological criticism are welcome — open an issue naming the doc id and
paragraph, or send a pull request. A correction that cites the judgment beats one that cites another summary.
