# Mr D v Ms C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 877 "ង ១", Phnom Penh Municipal Court, 17 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Hon Sokhemrin) · first instance · Doc ID 143252

## 1. Facts
On 12 April 2016 Ms C1 and her son Mr C2 borrowed 15,000 USD from the lender's Sen Sok branch to expand their business, and had repaid all but 7,500 USD of principal. [Para 10][Para 20] On 17 January 2017 they borrowed a further 17,500 USD (seventeen thousand five hundred), paid principal and interest for eleven months, made one payment of 450 USD in February 2018 and then defaulted, leaving 21,791.63 USD of principal outstanding. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 22] The lender sued in case file 745 of 18 April 2018 to dissolve both agreements and recover the principal, contractual interest, a penalty of twice the interest and 6,000,000 riel in damages. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 6][Para 7]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the lender may dissolve both loan agreements and recover the whole outstanding principal where the borrowers defaulted on the repayment schedules agreed in each? [Para 28][Para 47]
2. Whether the stipulated rates of 1.8% and 1.4% a month and a penalty of twice the interest owed survive Article 585(a) of the Civil Code and the 2011 Prakas capping interest? [Para 33][Para 34][Para 38]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, lender):** he maintains his statement of 30 August 2018; the two contracts of 12 April 2016 and 17 January 2017 and their schedules show the advances and the terms, the borrowers stopped paying in February 2018 after a single 450 USD payment, and clause 5 entitles him to dissolution, 21,791.63 USD, interest at 1.8% and 1.4% a month, a double penalty and 6,000,000 riel. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 24][Para 28]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2, borrowers):** no submissions — summonses were served on them properly but they were absent without reason from the preparatory stage through to the oral hearing, so the court treated them as having admitted the plaintiff's statement. [Para 25][Para 27]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is largely allowed — both loan agreements are dissolved; the defendants must pay 21,791.63 USD (twenty-one thousand seven hundred and ninety-one dollars and sixty-three cents) with 18% annual interest and a 27% annual penalty from the date of default until full payment; the 6,000,000 riel damages claim is dismissed; point 2 alone is provisionally enforceable; costs fall on the defendants. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
Because the defendants stayed away throughout, the court entered judgment in their absence under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 27] On the first issue it found the two agreements and their schedules proved, held they had broken clause 5 by not paying to schedule, and dissolved both contracts under Articles 407 and 408 of the Civil Code. [Para 28][Para 29] On interest it accepted the principal and the claim in principle but applied Article 585(a), paragraph 3, of the Civil Code — a rate fixed above the statutory ceiling is void as to the excess — and reduced the rate to the 18% a year set by the 2011 Ministry of Justice Prakas and Article 17 of the law implementing the Civil Code. [Para 33][Para 34] On the double penalty it held the penalty could be folded into late interest but capped delay damages at 27% a year under Article 2 of the same Prakas. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] On the 6,000,000 riel claim it refused, because the parties had already fixed the measure of damages in the contract and counted it into late interest, since awarding it again would make them pay twice for the same default. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] Provisional execution was granted for the principal only, the claim being one of right over property. [Para 44][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contractual interest rate above the statutory ceiling is not void in its entirety: under Article 585(a) of the Civil Code only the excess is void, and the court substitutes the lawful maximum — 18% a year for interest and 27% a year for delay damages — so the obligation survives at the capped rate. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 38] A creditor who has already fixed liquidated damages in the contract, and recovered them through late interest, cannot also recover general damages for the same delay, since that would be double recovery on one breach. [Para 42][Para 43]

**Obiter dicta:** that a defendant absent without reason at every stage is taken to have admitted the plaintiff's statement, enabling judgment in absentia under Article 201, and that provisional execution is confined to a fixed principal sum and not extended to running interest. [Para 27][Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The two loans were made at different rates and on different principal slices — 1.8% a month on 7,500 USD and 1.4% a month on 14,291.63 USD — but the order applies a single 18% rate to the whole 21,791.63 USD, so the second tranche, contracted below the cap at 16.8% a year, is charged at a higher rate than was ever agreed or claimed. [Para 5][Para 49] The total principal is written 21,791.63 USD but glossed in words as "twenty-one thousand seven hundred" in the prayers and the orders, omitting the 791.63, while one passage gives the full rendering. [Para 5][Para 12][Para 48] The hearing is dated 18 June 2018 although the judgment was delivered on 17 July 2019 and the file was opened in April 2018, leaving a thirteen-month gap the text does not explain. [Para 1][Para 54] The interest and penalty orders run "from the date the defendants failed to pay" without naming that date, though the narrative fixes it in February 2018. [Para 11][Para 49][Para 50] Party names survive only as initials, the court is twice printed "ភ្នំរពញ", and the judge's name appears as "ហន សុខេមរិន" and "បាន សុខេមរិន" in the same document. [Para 1][Para 56]
