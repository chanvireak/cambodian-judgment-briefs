# Microfinance Institution D v Mr C1, Ms C2, Mr C3 and Ms C4 (Civil Judgment No. 950 "Ph", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 7 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Sam Bun Pov) · first instance · Doc ID 143254

## 1. Facts
On 28 February 2013 Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2 signed loan contract No. 000153 with the microfinance institution D for 30,000 US dollars (thirty thousand dollars) at 1.5% per month, repayable over 48 months on a schedule of the same date, with a contractual penalty of (sum due × rate × days late × 2 ÷ 30 days). [Para 12] Mr C3 and Ms C4, husband and wife, stood as guarantors (អ្នកធានាបំណុល). [Para 13][Para 17] After two reschedulings the debtors stopped paying on 20 October 2017 and did not resume despite repeated notices; the claim was filed on 26 April 2019 for 30,833 dollars principal, interest to full payment, penalty, 10,000 dollars damages and lawyer's fees. [Para 15][Para 10]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a contractual rate of 1.5% per month may be applied, as claimed, from October 2017 "until full payment", or must be cut off at the date the claim was filed? [Para 31]
2. Whether a claim of 10,000 dollars for loss of time and lawyer's fees is recoverable in full where the debtors merely failed to perform the loan contract? [Para 33][Para 34]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (institution D, creditor):** the four defendants signed the loan contract and the guarantee, the debtors fell into arrears repeatedly, obtained two revised payment schedules, then stopped paying altogether on 20 October 2017, causing it serious loss of time and service costs; it asked for the full principal, interest to full satisfaction, the contractual penalty and 10,000 dollars in damages, with provisional enforcement on the first two heads. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 11]
**Defendants (Mr C1, Ms C2, Mr C3, Ms C4):** Mr C3 alone appeared and conceded the facts — that he and his wife Ms C4 guaranteed the 30,000 dollar borrowing by Mr C1 and his wife from institution D under the contract of 28 February 2013. [Para 17] Mr C1, Ms C2 and Ms C4 did not appear although summonsed to the oral hearing. [Para 18]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the four defendants are ordered jointly to pay 30,833 dollars (thirty thousand eight hundred and thirty-three) principal; interest at 1.5% per month on that principal from October 2017 to 26 April 2019; late interest of 1,019.49 dollars (one thousand nineteen and forty-nine cents); and 3,000,000 riel (three million riel) in damages for loss of time; all further claims are dismissed, court costs fall on the defendants, and the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to head 1(a). [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]
The court found on the written contracts and the oral hearing that all four defendants had indeed signed the loan agreement for 30,000 dollars with the plaintiff, represented by Mr T D, and that Mr C3 admitted it. [Para 27][Para 28] The principal of 30,833 dollars was admitted by the defendants, so it was awarded as claimed. [Para 29][Para 30] The 1.5% monthly rate was a rate the parties had themselves fixed in the contract and the defendants had agreed to pay, so the court applied it. [Para 32] It refused, however, to let that interest run to full satisfaction: that would be unreasonable, and interest was therefore calculated only from October 2017 to 26 April 2019, the date the statement of claim was lodged. [Para 31] The 10,000 dollar claim for loss of time and lawyer's fees was excessive, and the court assessed damages by reference to the actual situation, awarding 3,000,000 riel instead. [Para 33][Para 34] Provisional enforcement was granted on the admitted principal alone, and costs were allocated under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 35][Para 36]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a loan contract fixes a monthly rate of interest, the court will enforce that agreed rate on the outstanding principal, but will not enforce a claim that interest run until full payment; interest is limited to the period ending on the date the claim is filed, and a penalty or damages claim that exceeds the loss actually occasioned is reduced to an amount assessed on the facts. [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the allocation of court costs is governed by Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure, a procedural matter independent of the substantive award. [Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative paragraph garbles the defendants' sexes and birth years — Ms C2 is recorded there as male and the birth years of Mr C2, Mr C3 and Ms C4 are transposed — while the party details at the head of the judgment give Ms C2 as female, born 06 July 1983, Mr C3 born 07 April 1972 and Ms C4 born 15 April 1975. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 37] The costs paragraph opens with a stray fragment ("to the plaintiff … address") before the address of institution D, apparently a dropped verb. [Para 42] The late interest of 1,019.49 dollars is awarded as a figure only; the judgment does not show the computation, and it does not follow the contract formula (sum due × rate × days late × 2 ÷ 30) that the claim pleaded. [Para 9][Para 39] Parties survive in the OCR only as initials (C1–C4, institution D, representative Mr T D); these are reported as redactions, not expanded.
