# Ms D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 973 "Ph", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 14 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Sam Bun Pov) · first instance · Doc ID 143255

## 1. Facts
On 10 September 2018 Ms D signed a deposit contract (កិច្ចសន្យាកក់ប្រាក់ទិញលក់អចលនវត្ថុ) to buy a plot at 35 dollars per square metre from the son of Ms C, paying him 3,000 dollars as deposit; the land carried a title certificate dated 30 June 2010 and lay in Kandal province, now part of Phnom Penh. [Para 8][Para 24] Ms C, the registered owner, is recorded as 77 years old at the head of the judgment. [Para 3] Ms D sued for an order that Ms C perform the contract by paying her 30,000 dollars, plus 10,000,000 riel (ten million riel) in lost time, expenses and lawyer's fees, and costs. [Para 5][Para 6]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a deposit contract for the sale of land signed by the owner's son binds the registered owner where no written authority of agency to sell or to receive money is produced? [Para 33][Para 36]
2. Whether a contract of sale can be found where the evidence shows only the buyer's intention to buy, and not the owner's consent, under Articles 515 and 516 of the Civil Code? [Para 34][Para 35]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, buyer):** she met the owner directly, inspected the original land plan, negotiated the price of 35 dollars per square metre face to face, and was told to pay the deposit to the owner's son as receiver; the contract was acknowledged by the village chief, and after signature a grandchild of the defendant telephoned to threaten her while the defendant said on a voice recording that she would stop selling. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 14]
**Defendant (Ms C, registered owner):** she never agreed to sell the land to Ms D; the contract was written by Ms D alone without her consent; she never authorised her son to sell or to take money from Ms D, and no copy of the deposit contract was ever given to her — so the claim should be dismissed as unfounded. [Para 17][Para 18]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim in the statement of claim of 30 October 2018 is dismissed in its entirety from this date, and court costs are borne by the plaintiff. [Para 42][Para 43]
The court examined the deposit contract, the certificate of ownership, the electronic messages between Ms D and Ms C's son and the voice recording on the USB stick. [Para 31] It accepted that a deposit was in fact paid with a sale of land in view, but held that there was no legal relationship on which the court could assess a seller's obligations towards a buyer. [Para 31] The reason was that the contract was not in the name of Ms C, who is the owner of the property that was its subject matter. [Para 32] No document showed that Ms C had given her son authority to represent her in selling the land. [Para 33] The messages and recording showed Ms D's intention to buy but no intention on the defendant's side to sell at 35 dollars per square metre — the cost of transfer and registration being on the buyer and unrelated to that price. [Para 34] The court therefore concluded that the parties had not agreed on the sale in the manner required for formation of a contract of sale under Articles 515 and 516 of the Civil Code. [Para 35] Lacking any legal document or clear evidence of the son's authority, the court applied Article 369(a), paragraph 1, of the Civil Code: an act done by a person without the right of representation, in the name of a representative of another, does not create effects for the rights-holder. [Para 36][Para 38] Having found the evidence and the legal basis insufficient on all four heads, it dismissed the claims. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contract for the sale of immovable property signed by a person who is neither the registered owner nor the holder of a written authority to represent her creates no enforceable obligation for the owner, because under Article 369(a) of the Civil Code an act done without power of representation produces no effect against the rights-holder; and a sale is not formed under Articles 515–516 where only the buyer's intention is evidenced and the owner's consent to the price is not. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 38]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the buyer's liability for transfer and registration costs is unrelated to the agreed price per square metre, so that her assumption that the price included those costs could not establish the seller's consent. [Para 34]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The defendant's age is given as 77 at the head of the judgment but as 70 in the narrative and in the reasoning. [Para 3][Para 8][Para 36] The plaintiff is 39 at the head but the contract is described as made by a woman of 38. [Para 1][Para 23] Paragraph 27 is drafted as if the court were granting the claim in full ("order the defendant … to pay 30,000 dollars, because she spoke directly with the owner"), and the rejection appears only from paragraph 31 onwards; the paragraph is read here as a recital of the plaintiff's contention, not a finding. [Para 27][Para 31] The recurring banner "this is a factual case and not a model judgment" is spliced into the middle of paragraphs 9, 21 and 34 by the OCR. [Para 9][Para 21][Para 34] The claim is for 30,000 dollars although the deposit recited is 3,000 dollars, and the judgment does not reconcile the two figures. [Para 5][Para 8] Parties survive only as initials (Ms D, Ms C, "the son of Ms C"); these are reported as redactions.
