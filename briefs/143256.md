# Microfinance Institution D v Ms C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 116.2 "Th", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 17 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Mam Chan Vannak) · first instance · Doc ID 143256

## 1. Facts
On 9 July 2014 Ms C1 (born 1966) and her husband Mr C2 (born 1957) signed loan contract No. [number illegible] with the microfinance institution D for 20,000 US dollars (twenty thousand dollars) at 2% per month over 48 months, repayable in monthly instalments on a schedule issued by the institution, and on the same date signed a guarantee contract pledging a certificate of ownership dated 14 June 2013 over land in Kampot, of which both were the registered owners. [Para 8][Para 9] They paid down the principal to 9,780 US dollars (nine thousand seven hundred and eighty dollars) and then stopped paying principal, interest or penalty from 15 September 2016, despite repeated invitations to settle. [Para 10][Para 17][Para 18]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a contractual default rate of 2% per month, and a penalty of 3% per month, may be enforced as agreed where they exceed the maximum rate permitted by Article 585 of the Civil Code? [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]
2. Whether a judgment on an admitted debt may be declared provisionally enforceable at the request of a microfinance institution? [Para 51]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (institution D, creditor):** the contract of 9 July 2014 is valid and uncoerced, the debtors took the 20,000 dollars and performed for a time before defaulting on 15 September 2016, and the institution invited them many times to settle; it claims the 9,780 dollars outstanding, 2% per month from 15 September 2016 until payment, a 3% monthly penalty, and 10,000 dollars in tax, costs, lost time and expenses. [Para 12][Para 19][Para 6]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2, debtors):** they admit they truly owe the money under the contract of 9 July 2014 and still wish to repay, asking one month's time to negotiate with the institution first; they do not accept the 10,000 dollars claimed outside the contract. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed — the defendants are ordered to repay 9,780 US dollars principal; interest at 18% per annum on that principal from 16 July 2017 until full payment; and 2,000,000 riel (two million riel) for lawyer's fees, travel and other expenses; all further claims are dismissed, costs fall on the defendants, and the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to head 1. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]
The court found the contract genuine and free of coercion, a synallagmatic agreement between the parties. [Para 34][Para 35] The debtors had received 20,000 dollars and performed their obligations for a period, but had paid nothing since 15 September 2016 notwithstanding the institution's repeated invitations. [Para 36][Para 37] The principal of 9,780 dollars was admitted, and the request for a month to negotiate was unsupported by any evidence and was rejected. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42] On interest, the court accepted that the parties had agreed 2% per month for breach, but held that such a rate exceeds the maximum permitted by Article 585 of the Civil Code, and reduced it to the statutory maximum. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] On the 10,000 dollar claim the court found that the default had genuinely affected the creditor's interests and caused lost time and procedural expense, and awarded 2,000,000 riel as the reasonable measure. [Para 49][Para 50] Provisional enforcement was granted under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, the creditor being a microfinance institution needing its capital for its business and the debt being admitted. [Para 51]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where parties to a loan fix a default rate of interest above the ceiling set by Article 585 of the Civil Code, the court will not enforce the agreed rate but will reduce it to the statutory maximum, while the acknowledged principal remains fully payable; and a claim for procedural and legal expenses that is grossly above the loss shown is scaled down to a reasonable sum rather than dismissed. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** a debtor's stated willingness to repay, and a bare request for time to negotiate, are not evidence and do not delay judgment where nothing is produced to support them. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning validates "the loan contract dated 26 February 2016", whereas the contract sued on and described throughout is dated 9 July 2014. [Para 46][Para 8] Interest is claimed from 15 September 2016 but awarded only from 16 July 2017, and the judgment does not explain the change of date. [Para 5][Para 56] The penalty head is truncated mid-sentence in the OCR ("a penalty at 3% per month on the sum of …"), and clause (a) of the claim omits the date from which the principal is said to fall due. [Para 4][Para 6] The certificate of ownership is dated 14 June 2013 in the narrative but 4 June 2013 in the evidence list. [Para 9][Para 32] The loan and guarantee numbers are left blank in the OCR. [Para 8][Para 9] Parties survive only as initials (Ms C1, Mr C2, institution D); these are reported as redactions.
