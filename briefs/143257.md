# Bank D v C1 and C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1084 "Ch", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 3 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ham Meng Se) · first instance · Doc ID 143257

## 1. Facts
Between 7 July 2016 and 25 December 2017 the defendants signed six loan contracts (កិច្ចសន្យាខ្ចីបរិភោគ) with Bank D — 1,750,000 dollars at 9% a year over 60 months; 3,000,000 dollars at 9% over 12 months; 19,012,500 dollars at 12% over 12 months; and three further contracts of 3,000,000, 1,500,000 and 1,000,000 dollars at 9% over 12 months, each with a repayment appendix. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] The bank's debt statement of 11 February 2019 put the outstanding principal at 25,620,100 dollars (twenty-five million six hundred and twenty thousand one hundred dollars). [Para 37][Para 65] The bank sued for that principal, contractual interest at 9% or 12% per tranche, penalty interest at twice the contractual rate, a "processing fee" (សោហ៊ុយទម្រង់ការ) of 0.01% per tranche, 500,000 dollars for legal services and lost time, costs, and provisional enforcement. [Para 5][Para 12][Para 19][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the bank may recover the whole principal shown in its own debt statement where the borrowers assert that 3,000,000 dollars was withheld for interest and a further 3,000,000 dollars was never disbursed? [Para 42][Para 88]
2. Whether penalty interest at twice the contractual rate, and a 0.01% processing fee, may be recovered in addition to the contractual interest on each tranche? [Para 89][Para 90]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Bank D, creditor):** the six contracts are in writing with repayment appendices and the debt statement of 11 February 2019 records 25,620,100 dollars of principal outstanding; each tranche carries its own contract rate, the contracts provide doubled interest by way of penalty, and a 0.01% processing fee is chargeable on each — with 500,000 dollars for legal service and lost time. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 89]
**Defendants (C1 and C2, borrowers):** they did borrow six times, totalling 29,262,500 dollars, but the bank held back 3,000,000 dollars for interest and a further 3,000,000 dollars of "air money" never released, so they actually received 23,262,500 dollars and have repaid 3,642,400 dollars, leaving only 19,620,100 dollars; they ask for a flat 9% on that sum and dismissal of the penalty and processing-fee heads. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed — the defendants are ordered jointly to pay 25,620,100 dollars principal; contractual interest on each tranche at the contract rate (9% or 12%) from the contractual default dates until full payment; penalty interest at twice the contract rate on the same tranches; a processing fee of 0.01% on each tranche; and 250,000 dollars (two hundred and fifty thousand dollars) for legal service and lost time; all further claims are dismissed, costs fall on the defendants, and heads 1 to 4 are provisionally enforceable. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99][Para 104][Para 111][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]
Procedurally, the defendants' answer of 16 August 2019 was withdrawn by the party on 23 August 2019 in order to negotiate, and so was not considered; service on their representative being regular, judgment in absentia could be given under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 84][Para 85] On the principal, the court found from the six contracts that the defendants had borrowed 29,262,500 dollars, and from the debt statement of 11 February 2019 that 25,620,100 dollars remained outstanding. [Para 86][Para 87] It rejected the contention that only 19,620,100 dollars was owed, because the account statements filed by the defendants' own representative carried the same figures as the bank's debt table — which showed the claim well founded under Article 578 of the Civil Code. [Para 88] Interest, penalty and processing fees were granted as claimed, without any rate being reduced. [Para 90] On the 500,000 dollar claim for legal service and lost time, fault and loss were found established and 250,000 dollars awarded. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 118] Provisional enforcement followed because delay would aggravate the loss, consistently with Article 196. [Para 94]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A borrower who asserts that part of a loan was never disbursed, or was withheld by the lender, must displace the lender's own accounts by evidence; where the account statements produced by the borrower's representative agree with the lender's debt table, the court will accept the lender's figure as the outstanding principal under Article 578 of the Civil Code, and contractual penalty interest and processing fees pleaded on the strength of the same contracts will be enforced as agreed. [Para 88][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** that an answer withdrawn in order to negotiate is treated as not made, so judgment may proceed in absentia under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure; and that a claim for legal service and lost time may be halved even where fault and loss are found, without the judgment stating the measure used. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 118]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The defendants' sexes and years of birth are scrambled in the party heading, which gives C1 as male born 1966 and then as female born 1979. [Para 2] The judgment does not make any finding on the borrowers' central allegation that 3,000,000 dollars was withheld for interest and a further 3,000,000 dollars of principal was never disbursed; the reasoning moves straight from the contracts to the bank's debt table, and the sums the defendants say they actually received (23,262,500 dollars) are left unanswered. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 86][Para 88] The written amount for 25,620,100 dollars is misread once as "twenty-five million six hundred and twenty-one hundred". [Para 87] The plaintiffs' three representatives appear as Mr T1 D, Mr T2 D and Ms T3 D throughout, and the six contract numbers are blank in the OCR. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 96] The banner "this is a factual case and not a model judgment" is spliced into paragraphs 7, 19, 31, 54, 76, 87, 96, 105 and 116. [Para 7][Para 19][Para 31]
