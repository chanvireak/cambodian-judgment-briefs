# Ms D1, Ms D2 and Ms D3 v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 1156 "K", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 13 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ouk Sovannarith) · first instance · Doc ID 143258

## 1. Facts
On 19 September 2018 Ms C signed three separate loan contracts with three lenders: 23,500 dollars (twenty-three thousand dollars) from Ms D1, 47,000 dollars (forty-seven thousand dollars) from Ms D2, and 20,148 dollars (twenty thousand one hundred and forty-eight dollars) from Ms D3, each repayable over 24 months with monthly instalments in the first year and the balance in the second, all carrying interest at 18% a year. [Para 15][Para 18][Para 21] By the time of suit Ms C had repaid 3,750 dollars to Ms D1, 5,000 dollars to Ms D2 and 6,700 dollars to Ms D3, leaving balances of 19,750, 42,000 and 13,448 dollars respectively. [Para 17][Para 20][Para 23] The three lenders sued jointly for 90,648 dollars with interest, plus 50,000 dollars for distress, lost time and expenses. [Para 14][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a lender on a written loan contract recovers the whole sum contracted, or only the balance remaining after crediting the part-payments actually made? [Para 28][Para 38]
2. Whether court costs must be borne wholly by a defendant who has defaulted, where the plaintiffs succeed on the principal but fail on most of their unliquidated damages claim? [Para 38]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiffs (Ms D1, Ms D2, Ms D3, lenders):** each signed a written loan contract with Ms C on 19 September 2018 and paid over the full sum; Ms C failed to pay to schedule, then avoided responsibility, and their attempts to make contact came to nothing — so they claim the principal, 18% annual interest from the date of lending until payment, and 50,000 dollars for distress, lost time and expenses. [Para 14][Para 36]
**Defendant (Ms C, borrower):** the judgment records no answer and no submissions from her; the only resistance on the record is the series of part-payments she did make, which the court deducted loan by loan. [Para 28][Para 31][Para 34]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Ms C is ordered to pay Ms D1 19,750 dollars with 18% a year on the principal from the date of lending until full payment; Ms D2 42,000 dollars with 18% a year; and Ms D3 13,448 dollars with 18% a year; and to pay each of the three plaintiffs 1,500 dollars (one thousand five hundred dollars) in damages for distress, lost time and expenses; all further claims are dismissed, and costs are borne 85% by the defendant and 15% by the plaintiffs. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]
The court worked through the three contracts one at a time. On the evidence of the contract of 19 September 2018 between Ms D1 and Ms C it found a debt of 23,500 dollars; crediting the 3,750 dollars repaid left 19,750 dollars, which Ms D1 could claim with 18% a year from the date of lending. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] The same method applied to Ms D2's contract of the same date: 47,000 dollars lent, 5,000 dollars repaid, 42,000 dollars recoverable with 18%. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] And to Ms D3's contract: 20,148 dollars lent, 6,700 dollars repaid, 13,448 dollars recoverable with 18%. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] On the 50,000 dollar damages claim the court held that the plaintiffs had already recovered part of their loss through the repayments and that only the principal was truly in issue, so it granted compensation for the principal alone, at 1,500 dollars each. [Para 38][Para 41] Because the plaintiffs had succeeded only in part, costs were apportioned under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure — 85% to the defendant, 15% to the plaintiffs. [Para 38][Para 42] Appeal lies within one month of service. [Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a written loan contract is proved and part-payments are documented, the court awards the outstanding balance only, with contractual interest at the agreed rate running on the principal from the date of lending; and where the plaintiffs succeed on the debt but fail on the greater part of an unliquidated damages claim, costs are apportioned between the parties under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure rather than laid wholly on the defaulting defendant. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 38][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** that a claim of 50,000 dollars for distress and lost time, in a debt action whose real object is repayment of principal, warrants only nominal compensation of 1,500 dollars per plaintiff. [Para 38][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The written-out amounts contradict the figures. The 19,750 dollar balance is spelled "four myun nine thousand seven hundred and fifty" (49,000) and the 13,448 dollar balance "four myun three thousand four hundred and forty-eight" (43,448), although the arithmetic on the pleaded figures (23,500 − 3,750 and 20,148 − 6,700) gives 19,750 and 13,448, which is what was awarded. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 28][Para 34] The claim of 90,648 dollars is simply the sum of the three contracts (23,500 + 47,000 + 20,148) and ignores the 15,450 dollars already repaid, so the pleading and the award do not match. [Para 14][Para 41] The judgment contains no section recording the defendant's contentions and does not state whether she appeared, so the absence of a defence is inferred, not recorded. [Para 24] The banner "this is a factual case and not a model judgment" is spliced into paragraphs 14, 22 and 35. [Para 14][Para 22][Para 35] Parties survive only as initials (Ms D1–D3, Ms C); these are reported as redactions.
