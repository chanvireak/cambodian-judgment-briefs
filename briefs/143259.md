# Mr D and his wife v Mr C and his wife (Civil Judgment No. 11.21 "Th", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 6 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Mam Chan Vannak) · first instance · Doc ID 143259

## 1. Facts
On 7 June 2018 the plaintiffs, a married couple, agreed to sell Mr C and his wife 344 m² of land in Phnom Penh held under a certificate of ownership dated 14 June 2017, for 60,000 dollars (sixty thousand dollars). [Para 13] Mr C paid 10,000 dollars (ten thousand dollars) as a deposit so that the sellers would carry the transfer of title into his name, and promised the remaining 50,000 dollars (fifty thousand dollars) once the transfer was complete. [Para 13][Para 29] On 18 July 2018 the sellers completed the transfer and repeatedly asked Mr C to come and collect the original title deed; he never came and never responded. [Para 14][Para 32] The sellers sued for dissolution of the agreement, retention of the deposit, re-transfer of the title and damages. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 10]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a seller who has already transferred title may dissolve the sale and retain the deposit where the buyer fails to pay the balance and refuses to take delivery of the deed? [Para 35][Para 37][Para 48]
2. Whether a defendant who stays away throughout the proceedings and files no evidence is to be taken as having admitted the plaintiff's account, so that the court may order the title re-registered in the seller's name? [Para 43][Para 44]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiffs (Mr D and his wife, sellers):** the agreement of 7 June 2018 was for 60,000 dollars with a 10,000 dollar deposit to cover the transfer; they performed it fully on 18 July 2018 and notified Mr C many times to collect the original deed, but he neither came nor answered; the agreement itself was taken away by Mr C, so they rely on two witnesses (a man of 72 and a woman of 48) and the commune chief's attestation of 20 April 2019, and ask that the witnesses be heard. [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19][Para 24][Para 28][Para 30]
**Defendants (Mr C and his wife, buyers):** absent from beginning to end; they filed no documents and made no submissions, and the judgment records no sign that they contested anything. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 43]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the sale agreement of 7 June 2018 is declared dissolved; the plaintiffs are allowed to retain the 10,000 dollar deposit; Mr C is ordered to register the transfer of the land back to the plaintiffs and the Phnom Penh land department to register that re-transfer; Mr C is to bear the costs of re-registration; and he is ordered to pay 10,000,000 riel (ten million riel) in damages for distress, lost time and legal service; heads 1 to 4 are provisionally enforceable and costs fall on the defendant. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
The court found the sale and the deposit proved by the plaintiffs' account, the two witnesses and the certificate of ownership, which bore an endorsement showing that title had in fact passed from the plaintiffs to Mr C by sale. [Para 31][Para 33] Notice to collect the deed and pay the balance had reached Mr C through the village chief's telephone call. [Para 34] His conduct was a serious breach, because the plaintiffs had warned him and given a reasonable time to perform and he did nothing. [Para 35][Para 36] The plaintiffs could therefore dissolve the contract under Articles 407 and 408 of the Civil Code. [Para 37] Dissolution obliges the parties to make restitution to one another, so the title had to go back and Mr C had to bear the cost of the re-transfer. [Para 38][Para 51] On damages, the failure had genuinely affected the plaintiffs' interests and caused lost time and expense, and the court assessed compensation on the facts at 10,000,000 riel against the 50,000 dollars claimed. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 52] Provisional enforcement was granted under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure as a claim over property. [Para 41][Para 42] Because the defendant had been served at every stage and had neither appeared nor contested, he was treated as having confessed the plaintiff's account under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 43][Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a buyer of land has obtained registration of title but fails to pay the balance and ignores the seller's notices, the seller may dissolve the sale under Articles 407 and 408 of the Civil Code, keep the deposit, and obtain restitution — re-registration of the property in his own name at the buyer's expense — while damages for the breach are assessed on the facts rather than at the sum pleaded. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 48][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** that the written sale agreement need not itself be in court where the buyer has carried the original away, its making being provable by two witnesses and the commune chief's attestation. [Para 18][Para 24]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The judgment records the disputed issues as "none", although the whole claim was contested by absence and the court then decided several contested heads. [Para 23] The case-number date is printed as "5 June 2091" in the opening paragraph and as 2019 elsewhere. [Para 1] The certificate of ownership is dated "14 June 017" in the operative order and 2017 in the narrative. [Para 25][Para 49] The plaintiffs' names are lost: the party heading splices the clerk's name (Mr Chhoeun Sok N) into the plaintiffs' line, and the sellers appear only as Mr D and his wife; the defendant appears only as Mr C and his wife. [Para 1][Para 3] The deposit is spelled "one myun" ("មួយ មួយ ម៉ឺន") in three places, an OCR duplication. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 31] The witnessed statements are badly scrambled mid-sentence, with the parties' names and the land dimensions transposed. [Para 28][Para 30]
