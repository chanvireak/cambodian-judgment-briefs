# Mr D v Mr C (Civil Judgment No. 1125 "S", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 9 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Theam Chan Piseth) · first instance · Doc ID 143261

## 1. Facts
On 9 January 2017 Mr C sold Mr D a 160 m² plot in Phnom Penh under a written contract of sale, the plot being bounded north by a road, south by "name 1" and east and west by "name 2". [Para 5][Para 13] Mr C's own title rested on a letter of transfer of rights of management and use of land (លិខិតផ្ទេរសិទ្ធិគ្រប់គ្រង និងប្រើប្រាស់ដីធ្លី) of 13 September 2016 from the previous holder, who had acquired the plot in January 2016 by a transfer letter of the district governor dated 19 January 2016. [Para 7][Para 11][Para 12] Mr D has possessed the plot ever since and sued for recognition of ownership, delivery, and registration in his name. [Para 8]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the court will recognise a buyer as owner of land on the strength of a chain of administrative transfer letters, where the vendor concedes the sale and the plaintiff's possession? [Para 13][Para 14]
2. Whether orders for delivery of the property and for registration of title may be made against a vendor who has agreed to the claim in its entirety? [Para 10][Para 14]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, buyer):** he bought the 160 m² plot from Mr C by written contract of 9 January 2017, Mr C holding it under the district governor's transfer letter of 2016 and the transfer of 13 September 2016; he has occupied it continuously and asks the court to declare him owner, order Mr C to hand the land over, and order registration in his name. [Para 8][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13]
**Defendant (Mr C, vendor):** he confirms that on 9 January 2017 he sold the plot to Mr D, that he held it under the district governor's letter of 19 January 2016, and that he agrees wholly with the claim as pleaded. [Para 9][Para 10]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Mr D is recognised as owner of the 160 m² plot; Mr C is ordered to deliver the land to him; Mr C is ordered to carry out the registration of the land in Mr D's name; and court costs fall on Mr C. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]
The court took the three documents in sequence. The district governor's letter of transfer of 19 January 2016 showed the plot to be the property of "name 3", with the boundaries recited in the claim. [Para 11] The transfer letter of 13 September 2016 showed that "name 3" had passed the right to deal with the land to Mr C. [Para 12] The sale contract of 9 January 2017 showed that Mr C had then sold the plot to Mr D, together with the district governor's transfer letter. [Para 13] Because Mr C accepted the claim in full, the court held it should decide in accordance with that agreement, and so granted recognition, delivery and registration. [Para 10][Para 14] Costs were allocated to the defendant under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 15][Para 18]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a vendor concedes the claim in its entirety and the documentary chain — an administrative letter of transfer of rights of management and use, a subsequent transfer to the vendor, and a written contract of sale to the plaintiff — supports the buyer's case, the court will declare the buyer owner, order delivery of the land and order registration in his name without any further inquiry into the vendor's own title. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]

**Obiter dicta:** that costs are laid on a defendant who has conceded the whole claim, under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 15][Para 18]
