# Ms D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 960 Ch, Phnom Penh First Instance Court, 09 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ko Vandy, deputy president) · first instance · Doc ID 143267

## 1. Facts
Ms C bought jewellery from Ms D and did not pay, and on 12 January 2014 signed a debt-acknowledgment contract (កិច្ចសន្យាទទួលស្គាល់បំណុល) admitting a debt of USD 740,936 (seven hundred and forty thousand nine hundred and thirty-six US dollars), repayable in instalments over 15 years from 12 January 2014 to 12 December 2028. [Para 12][Para 13] As security Ms C issued 180 bank cheques (មូលប្បទានប័ត្រ) of USD 5,656 each for Ms D to cash monthly, and the cheques presented on 12 February 2018 and 12 March 2018 were dishonoured for insufficient funds. [Para 14][Para 20][Para 21] Ms D sued on 24 April 2018 for the whole balance and USD 200,000 in damages; a provisional attachment order No. 183 Ch-Dor of 16 May 2018 was issued in the proceedings. [Para 8][Para 36][Para 27]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether Ms D may claim the entire balance of USD 740,936 at once when the debt-acknowledgment contract allowed repayment over 15 years, because the security cheques were dishonoured? [Para 43][Para 44]
2. Whether the disputed USD 260,936 above the USD 480,000 Ms C admitted is owed, and whether USD 200,000 in damages is justified? [Para 29][Para 30][Para 45]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff and counter-defendant (Ms D, seller):** the acknowledgment contract of 12 January 2014 recorded the debt for jewellery already delivered; clause 5 of that contract let her claim the whole sum at once where the security cheques failed, and the February and March 2018 cheques did fail, so she claimed USD 740,936 plus USD 200,000 in damages. [Para 16][Para 43][Para 44]
**Defendant and counter-claimant (Ms C, buyer):** she bought the jewellery but never paid; she admitted USD 480,000 and disputed the remaining USD 260,936, saying the true price was about USD 400,000 and that nothing proved the higher figure, and she asked the court to enforce the instalment contract and to lift the attachment so she could pay. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 39]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Ms C is ordered to pay Ms D USD 740,936 (seven hundred and forty thousand nine hundred and thirty-six US dollars) and USD 80,000 (eighty thousand US dollars) in damages, moral injury and procedural expenses, with everything claimed beyond that dismissed; point 1 is provisionally enforceable and costs fall on Ms C. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]
The court first held the plaint of 24 April 2018 admissible under Article 9 of the Code of Civil Procedure, then recorded that Ms C had received the summons for the first and second preparatory hearings but deliberately stayed away, which under the article cited at Article 96 of the Code is treated as an admission of the facts asserted by the other party, leaving no dispute on the plaint. [Para 36][Para 22] On the documents it accepted the acknowledgment contract of 12 January 2014 as proof of the debt, found Ms C to be the buyer within the Civil Code, and held under Article 555(a) paragraph 1 that a buyer must pay the price at the time and place agreed, which she had not done. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] Clause 5 of the contract made her liable where the security cheques could not be cashed, and since two of them carried no funds, Ms D was entitled to sue for the entire balance. [Para 43][Para 44] The court rejected Ms C's assertion that the price was only about USD 400,000 for want of any evidence, and awarded USD 80,000 instead of the USD 200,000 claimed for moral injury, dismissing the excess. [Para 39][Para 45][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A debtor who signs a debt-acknowledgment contract fixing both the sum and a long repayment schedule is bound by the sum acknowledged, and where the security cheques given under that contract are dishonoured the creditor may accelerate and claim the entire balance at once; the debtor's unexplained absence from the preparatory hearings is treated as an admission of the creditor's factual assertions, and general damages are cut down to a reasonable figure. [Para 22][Para 43][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a buyer's obligation to pay the price at the agreed time and place, under Article 555(a) paragraph 1 of the Civil Code, is what grounds the award once the sale itself is admitted. [Para 42]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The court cites "Article 96" of the Code of Civil Procedure for the deemed-admission rule, and the passage it quotes is the rule on a party who fails to state its position in the preparatory or hearing stages. [Para 22] Ms C's admission of USD 480,000 leaves USD 260,936 in dispute, yet the acknowledgment contract is the only document relied on for that balance, and no calculation of it appears. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 40] The caption runs the case number, judgment number and dates together ("លេខ ៩៦០ \"ឆ\" ៨៤៧ ចុះ ថ្ងៃទី ០៤ ខែឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៨"), and para 8 breaks off mid-amount. [Para 1][Para 8] Party names survive only as "ដ" and "ច", and the operative order prints "ឈ្មោះ សុដ" as a fragment of the plaintiff's name. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 48]
