# Ms D v Mr C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1055 B, Phnom Penh First Instance Court, 29 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Hong Sokun Vathana) · first instance · Doc ID 143268

## 1. Facts
On 01 March 2013 Ms D transferred USD 100,000 into Mr C2's personal bank account, and on 06 March 2013 Mr C1 signed a loan contract secured by a mortgage over land and a house of 1,050 m² held under a transfer letter of 13 October 2005, with interest fixed at USD 5 per tonne of dried cassava and repayment due on 05 September 2013. [Para 19][Para 18][Para 15] Part of that land had already been sold on to others from 2007 and 253 m² transferred to Mr C2 on 10 January 2007, leaving Mr C1 holding 797 m². [Para 20] Nothing was repaid, and Ms D's fraud complaint ended with conviction at first instance (No. 643 K1, 23 March 2017) and acquittal on appeal (No. 96 KII-N, 20 September 2018), the Appeal Court holding the matter civil. [Para 16][Para 21]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a loan of USD 100,000 was in fact made, where the borrower admits signing the mortgage loan contract but denies receiving the money? [Para 61][Para 65]
2. Whether Mr C2, who thumbprinted the contract only as a witness, is jointly liable, and what interest and damages are recoverable when the agreed interest is expressed per tonne of cassava? [Para 68][Para 71][Para 73]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, lender):** the mortgage loan contract of 06 March 2013 and the receipt of the same date record a USD 100,000 loan repayable on 05 September 2013 at USD 5 per tonne of dried cassava; she claimed the principal, 18% annual interest from 06 March 2014 and USD 30,000 in damages. [Para 18][Para 24][Para 25]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Mr C2):** Mr C1 admitted signing the contract but said he never received the money and that the document secured a joint cassava venture rather than a loan; Mr C2 said she was a joint venturer who borrowed from a bank to fund it, received nothing, and signed only as a witness. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 52][Para 54]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Mr C1 alone is ordered to pay USD 100,000 (one hundred thousand US dollars) principal, interest at 5% per year from 06 March 2014 until payment in full, and 40,000,000 (forty million) riel in damages and lawyer's fees; the claim against Mr C2 is dismissed, provisional enforcement is refused, and costs fall on Mr C1. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]
The court found the contract and the contemporaneous handover receipt of 06 March 2013 proved that Mr C1 had borrowed and received the USD 100,000. [Para 65][Para 61] It rejected his account that he never received the money because it went into Mr C2's account, since the deposit was made on 01 March 2013 and the loan contract was signed on 06 March 2013, an order of events that made no sense on his version. [Para 66] As to Mr C2, the court held the contract showed her thumbprint in the capacity of witness only, and no evidence showed she owed anything, so the claim against her was dismissed. [Para 68][Para 69] The agreed interest of USD 5 per tonne of cassava being difficult to turn into a figure, the court applied the legal rate and fixed 5% per year from 06 March 2014. [Para 71][Para 72] The USD 30,000 damages claim was unsupported by any evidence of loss, so the court awarded 40,000,000 riel instead. [Para 73][Para 82] Provisional enforcement was refused because the defendants had contested the claim at every procedural stage, which put the certainty of the debt in doubt. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 84]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A borrower who signs a loan contract and a contemporaneous receipt cannot defeat the claim by denying receipt, and a person who thumbprints the contract in the capacity of witness only is not a co-debtor; where the agreed interest is expressed in kind and cannot be quantified, the court substitutes the legal rate, and provisional enforcement is refused where the defendant has contested the claim throughout. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 72][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that an earlier criminal acquittal on the ground that the dispute was civil does not bar the civil claim. [Para 21][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The court awards interest at "5% per year" after saying the legal rate applies, but cites no provision for that figure, while the plaint claimed 18% and the contract fixed USD 5 per tonne. [Para 24][Para 71][Para 81] The passage recording the Appeal Court's decision is garbled and attaches a sum of USD 120,000 to it, which conflicts with the claim of USD 100,000. [Para 22] Several passages are badly broken, including "ការ មិន បាន ដឹង ឬ ។" standing as a whole paragraph and a sentence naming the deposit date in the middle of the parties' names. [Para 37][Para 36] Party names survive only as "ដ", "ច១" and "ច២", and the mortgage contract's number and the land title numbers are blank. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 18]
