# Bank (name redacted) v Mr C1, Ms C1-2, Mr C2 and Ms C2-2 (Civil Judgment No. 890 "Ch", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 22 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Ham Mengse) · first instance · Doc ID 143270

## 1. Facts
On 27 June 2016 the four defendants signed a loan contract (កិច្ចសន្យាចងការប្រាក់) with the plaintiff bank for USD 121,173 at 12% per annum over 72 months, repayable at a fixed USD 2,380 a month from 27 July 2016 to 27 June 2022, on the security of two mortgage contracts of 17 August 2012 and 10 July 2013. [Para 11][Para 21][Para 22] They paid only two instalments — 07 August and 07 September 2016 — leaving USD 118,905.59 outstanding, and the bank sued on 13 November 2018. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]
Mr C1 and Ms C1-2 appeared and admitted the debt; Mr C2 and Ms C2-2 never appeared at any stage although the registry served them lawfully. [Para 15][Para 20]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the bank's claim for the outstanding principal and contractual interest is legally founded where the appearing borrowers admit the debt? [Para 21][Para 28]
2. Whether penalty interest at twice the contractual rate, and damages for legal fees, lost time and distress, may be awarded in addition to that principal and interest? [Para 32][Para 33]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank, represented by Mr T D):** the contract binds all four signatories; after two instalments the defendants stopped paying altogether, so it claims USD 118,905.59 principal, 12% annual interest from 07 October 2016, penalty interest at twice the contractual rate from 07 September 2016, and USD 20,000 for legal fees, lost time and emotional distress. [Para 13][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]
**Defendants Mr C1 and Ms C1-2 (co-borrowers, husband and wife):** they admit the loan and the principal with 12% interest, but ask to be excused penalty interest, legal fees and damages because they are victims too — the money was received in full by Mr C2 and Ms C2-2, and they were merely named as co-borrowers; they ask for four months to complete a land transfer so as to raise the money, and for the secured property to be sold first. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is largely allowed. All four defendants are ordered jointly to pay USD 118,905.59 principal, 12% annual interest on it from 07 October 2016, penalty interest of 24% a year from 07 September 2016, and 10,000,000 (ten million) riel in damages; the remainder of the claim is dismissed, costs lie on the defendants, and points 1(a), (b) and (c) are provisionally enforceable. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]
The court took the loan contract as the law of the parties and, reading it with the amortisation table of 04 September 2018, found the borrowing of USD 121,173 and the two payments proved; Mr C1 and Ms C1-2 had admitted the outstanding balance and shown no capacity to pay, so the principal claim was well founded under Article 578 of the Civil Code. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] Contractual interest followed from the same contract and from the defendants' admission of the interest term at the preparatory hearing. [Para 31] Penalty interest was allowed because the defendants' failure to perform their contractual obligation caused the bank loss of its expected benefit — the court treating the stipulated double rate as a proper basis. [Para 32] On the USD 20,000 claim the court accepted fault and loss in principle but assessed compensation "according to the actual situation" at 10,000,000 riel, dismissing the excess. [Para 33][Para 39] Provisional enforcement was granted under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, since waiting for the judgment to become final would aggravate the bank's loss, and costs were fixed under Article 64 K, first paragraph, of that Code. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A co-borrower who signed the loan contract and admits the outstanding balance remains jointly liable for principal, contractual interest and penalty interest; the fact that a different co-borrower actually received the funds is not a defence, because the obligation arises from the signature and the default, not from receipt of the money. [Para 18][Para 29][Para 32]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that lawful service on the non-appearing defendants allowed the court to adjudicate their liability in their absence, and the rationale that interim enforceability is justified wherever delay would aggravate the creditor's loss before the judgment becomes final. [Para 20][Para 34]
