# Bank (name redacted) v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 480 "5", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 18 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Hong Sokun Vaddhana) · first instance · Doc ID 143272

## 1. Facts
Two loans were taken from the plaintiff bank: USD 27,000 under a contract of 23 June 2016 and USD 10,000 under a contract of 11 April 2017, both at 1.2% interest a month with a 24% a year penalty rate. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 19] On 30 November 2017 the borrowers asked to consolidate the two facilities, leaving a principal of USD 33,260.07; they made one further payment of USD 478.95 and then stopped paying from 05 January 2018. [Para 15][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]
The bank sued for USD 33,260.07 principal, 1.2% a month interest, 24% a year penalty, USD 4,000 for lost time and distress, and provisional enforcement. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the borrowers' denial that they received the full principal — they say only USD 26,730 and USD 9,900 were disbursed — displaces the signed loan contracts and the consolidated balance? [Para 29][Para 44][Para 48]
2. Whether the 1.2% monthly interest and 24% annual penalty stand where the borrowers say the bank never explained the penalty and other banks charge only 0.6%–0.9% a month? [Para 36][Para 39][Para 53]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank, represented by Mr T D):** the consolidated principal is USD 33,260.07; nothing has been paid since 05 January 2018, so principal, contractual interest, penalty and USD 4,000 damages are all due. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 24]
**Defendants (borrowers, husband and wife, through counsel Meas Samith):** they deny the loans as pleaded — the contracts totalled USD 37,000 but only USD 26,730 and USD 9,900 (USD 36,630) were actually disbursed, and USD 32,890.07 remains; the penalty rate was never explained to them so they knew only the 1.2% monthly interest; that rate is higher than the 0.6%, 0.7% and 0.9% other banks charge; the USD 4,000 damages are unagreed and should be refused; they also ask for time to sell their house and, in a statement of 02 July 2019, asked that the plaint be struck out because no document proved Mr T D's authority to represent the bank. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 34][Para 36][Para 38][Para 41][Para 64]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed. The defendants are ordered jointly to pay USD 33,260.07 principal, 1.2% a month interest on it from 05 January 2018, 24% a year penalty on it from 05 January 2018, and 2,000,000 (two million) riel in damages for lost time and distress; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal up to USD 32,890.07; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]
The court found the parties' statements consistent with the two loan contracts and with the documentary evidence, and rejected the defendants' denial of receipt as unsupported; it held they had produced nothing to discharge their burden and that the outstanding principal was therefore USD 33,260.07. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 54][Para 55] On interest and penalty, the court held the defendants had signed the contracts and so agreed to their terms, and that the 1.2% and 24% rates therefore applied as written. [Para 53] The USD 4,000 distress claim was disallowed as to quantum because no evidence proved the extent of the loss, and the court substituted 2,000,000 riel. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 72] Provisional enforcement was restricted to the principal, under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, because counsel's denial of the balance raised a doubt over the exact debt. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63] The late application to strike out the plaint failed: a letter of appointment of 03 July 2017 authorised Mr T D to act for the plaintiff, so the challenge was dismissed under Articles 52, 53 and 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A borrower who signed the loan contract cannot escape the stipulated interest and penalty rates by asserting that they were not explained or that other banks lend more cheaply; the written contract governs unless the terms are shown to be vitiated. Where, however, a claim for lost time and distress is pleaded in a lump sum without proof of quantum, the court will assess a modest sum in riel rather than award the sum claimed. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a doubt over the exact sum owed, raised by the defendant's denial, justifies limiting interim enforceability to the principal. [Para 60][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning states at line 49 that no evidence shows the defendants ever paid anything, yet the recitals record payments of USD 4,691.67 principal, USD 3,166.36 interest and USD 129.61 penalty (USD 9,251.72 in all) and a further USD 478.95 after consolidation. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 49] The judgment orders payment of USD 33,260.07 but makes the judgment provisionally enforceable up to USD 32,890.07, the figure the defendants admitted. [Para 69][Para 73] The interest and penalty are both said to run "from 05 January 2018, from the day of borrowing", which cannot be right since the loans date from 2016 and 2017. [Para 9][Para 10] The authorising letter comes from a micro-finance institution ("គ្រឹះស្ថានមីក្រូហិរញ្ញវត្ថុ ដ") while the plaintiff is described as a bank; the document and case numbers are blank throughout; and party names survive only as initials. [Para 5][Para 65]
