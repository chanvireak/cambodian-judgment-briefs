# Micro-finance institution (name redacted) v Ms C1, Mr C3 and Ms C4 (Civil Judgment No. 1521 "Ch1", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 28 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Ham Mengse, Vice-President) · first instance · Doc ID 143273

## 1. Facts
On 24 March 2014 four borrowers — Ms C1, her husband Mr C2, and their children Mr C3 and Ms C4 — borrowed USD 25,000 from the plaintiff institution at 1.5% interest a month over 60 months, secured by a pledge of property, and signed a separate security contract the same day. [Para 14][Para 27][Para 28] Payments stopped in January 2016; by the date of suit, 20 August 2019, USD 14,277.66 of principal, USD 7,017.53 of interest and USD 9,853.51 of penalty were said to be outstanding, totalling USD 31,148.70. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]
Mr C2 had died in 2014, as a commune death certificate records; only Ms C1 appeared, the two children being absent though summoned. [Para 4][Para 24][Para 33]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a contractual interest rate of 1.5% a month — 18% a year — is lawful, and whether penalty interest of 27% a year may be charged on the aggregate of principal, interest and unpaid penalty? [Para 37][Para 38]
2. Whether the borrowers' admission of the principal, coupled with their plea of inability to pay until they sell their house, defeats the claim for interest, penalty and damages? [Para 40][Para 41]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the institution, represented by Mr T D, with counsel Koy Kanthy and Saret Sarendy):** the loan contract of 24 March 2014 governs; USD 31,148.70 is due to the filing date, with penalty at 27% a year running on that aggregate until payment, plus USD 10,000 for damage and attorney fees, and provisional enforcement. [Para 20][Para 19]
**Defendants (borrowers; Ms C1 appearing for herself and purporting to speak for her absent children):** she admits the principal of USD 14,277.66 and asks to pay it once she has sold her house, saying she has no means to pay the interest, the penalty or the lawyer's fees. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 40][Para 41]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants Ms C1, Mr C3 and Ms C4 are ordered jointly to pay USD 14,277.66 principal, USD 7,017.53 interest to 20 August 2019, USD 9,853.51 penalty, penalty interest of 27% a year on the aggregate USD 31,148.70 from 20 August 2019 until payment, and USD 1,000 in damages; the rest of the claim is dismissed; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal only; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]
The plaint and the authority of Mr T D and of both counsel were held regular. [Para 31] On the principal, the court noted that the defendants admitted the USD 14,277.66 claimed and decided the point on the parties' agreement. [Para 36] On interest, it converted the 1.5% monthly rate into 18% a year and held that this matched the ceiling of 18% a year fixed by Ministry of Justice Prakas No. 128 K.Y. Prak/11 of 21 December 2011, so the interest claim was lawful. [Para 37] The accrued penalty of USD 9,853.51 and the continuing penalty of 27% a year on the aggregate were also held founded on the contract. [Para 38] The court took note of the USD 10,000 claim for damage and attorney fees and, without explaining the reduction, awarded USD 1,000. [Para 39][Para 49] The defendants' plea that they could not pay was held to be no answer in law. [Para 41] Mr C2, having died in 2014, was not included in the order to pay, although the judgment does not discuss his estate or the liability of his heirs. [Para 33][Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A stipulated monthly interest rate is tested against the statutory ceiling by converting it to an annual rate: 1.5% a month is 18% a year and is therefore lawful under Prakas No. 128 of 21 December 2011. A borrower's inability to pay, and a wish to await the sale of a house, do not reduce the contractual interest, penalty or costs; only the sum claimed for damage and attorney fees, which is not quantified by evidence, is left to the court's assessment. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41][Para 39]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the case was not complex and could be tried by a single judge. [Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The same death certificate is dated 13 October 2014 at lines 4 and 30 but 16 December 2007 at line 33, and the land transfer letter is dated 11 December 2007 at line 30 and 16 December 2007 at line 34. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 34] The aggregate claimed is printed as "5.7" at line 38, the leading digits having dropped, though it is USD 31,148.70 elsewhere. [Para 38] Lines 53–54 are badly disordered, mixing the pronouncement formula with the judge's name and seal. [Para 53][Para 54] The judgment does not address the effect of Mr C2's death on a debt incurred in March 2014 and left unpaid from January 2016, yet simply omits him from the order. [Para 33][Para 45] All party names are reduced to initials and every contract number and address is blank.
