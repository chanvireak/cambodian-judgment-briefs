# Ms D v Mr C (Civil Judgment No. 1383 "Nh", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 31 October 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Ly Sok Leng) · first instance · Doc ID 143276

## 1. Facts
On 18 July 2012 Ms D lent Mr C USD 10,000 (one ten thousand) by a written loan contract that charged no interest and fixed repayment for 01 September 2012. [Para 10][Para 16] Nothing was repaid, and on 29 January 2013 the parties signed a second agreement under which Mr C undertook to repay by instalments of USD 200 a month from 01 February 2013. [Para 13][Para 17] He never made a single instalment, and Ms D sued on 22 March 2013 for the USD 10,000, 8,000,000 riel for distress, and costs. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 11]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the loan contract of 18 July 2012 is valid and whether the USD 10,000 was actually handed over, the borrower denying receipt while admitting his signature? [Para 16][Para 19]
2. Whether the borrower may have both contracts rescinded under Articles 407 and 408 of the Civil Code on the sole ground that he never received the money? [Para 21][Para 27]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, lender, through counsel (name redacted)):** the money was handed over in the presence of a witness, the borrower signed both the loan contract and the repayment agreement of 29 January 2013, and he has paid nothing despite his promise of USD 200 a month. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]
**Defendant (Mr C, borrower, through counsel Oeung Rithy):** all claims should be dismissed and both the loan contract of 18 July 2012 and the repayment agreement of 29 January 2013 rescinded, because he never received the USD 10,000 — he only signed the loan contract. [Para 6][Para 15][Para 19]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Mr C is ordered to pay USD 10,000 principal and 3,000,000 (three million) riel in damages for distress; the balance of the 8,000,000 riel claim is dismissed, as is his counterclaim for rescission; he bears the costs. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]
The court held the loan contract of 18 July 2012 valid on its terms — USD 10,000, no interest, repayment on 01 September 2012 — and found the default established. [Para 20] On the denial of receipt, it reasoned that the defendant had produced neither witness nor document to show he did not receive the money, whereas the repayment agreement of 29 January 2013, which he signed, recorded both that he had borrowed and received USD 10,000 and his undertaking to repay USD 200 a month from 01 February 2013; that agreement therefore operated as an acknowledgment of the debt and of receipt. [Para 19][Para 20] The request for rescission was refused for want of any ground under Articles 407 and 408 of the Civil Code. [Para 21][Para 27] Distress was found proved in principle because the breach caused loss, and was assessed "according to the actual situation" at 3,000,000 riel rather than the 8,000,000 claimed. [Para 22][Para 25] Costs followed the defendant's fault under Article 64 K, first paragraph, of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 23][Para 26]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A borrower who signed the loan contract and afterwards signed a repayment agreement acknowledging the sum and promising monthly instalments cannot defeat the claim by a bare denial of receipt; the later agreement is an acknowledgment of both the debt and the disbursement, and the burden of proving non-receipt lies on him. Rescission under Articles 407–408 of the Civil Code requires a ground, which a denial of receipt alone does not supply. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that an award for distress may be reduced to a sum the court considers real, irrespective of the amount pleaded. [Para 22][Para 25]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The defendant's counterclaim is recorded as seeking rescission of a loan contract "dated 18 July 2015" at line 6, while every other reference, including the evidence list, gives 18 July 2012. [Para 6][Para 16] The judgment states at line 1 that the oral reasoning was opened on 31 October 2019, but at line 29 that it was closed on 14 October 2019 and published on 31 October 2019. [Para 1][Para 29] The reasoning at line 20 is syntactically inverted, reading that the defendant "has no witness or evidence proving that he did not receive the money" as the reason to disregard his denial. [Para 20] Line 24 joins the parties' initials into a single garbled string ("C D ... named"), and line 27 repeats a date twice. [Para 24][Para 27] Only initials survive for the parties and the witness (Ms S D); addresses, identity-card numbers and occupations are blank.
