# Bank D v Ms C1, Mr C2 and Mr C3 (Civil Judgment No. 1114 "ក១", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 5 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Than Leang) · first instance · Doc ID 143279

## 1. Facts
On 25 June 2018 Bank D lent Ms C1, Mr C2 and Mr C3 USD 34,000 over 60 months under a medium-term credit contract at 10% a year, secured by a mortgage over unregistered immovable property and a transfer-of-possession letter dated 20 April 2018. [Para 14][Para 35] The borrowers serviced two instalments only — USD 2,033.88 in principal and interest — and then defaulted despite written reminders, leaving USD 32,566.66 of principal and USD 1,954.48 of accrued interest outstanding at 26 April 2019. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 38]
The bank sued for the balance, 15% a year post-maturity interest, 25% a year damages for breach, USD 5,000 in mental distress, provisional enforcement and costs. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the defendants owe the principal of USD 32,566.66 and accrued interest of USD 1,954.48? [Para 34][Para 41]
2. Whether the claimed 15% annual post-maturity interest and 25% annual breach damages are enforceable, or must be cut down to the statutory interest ceiling? [Para 39]
3. Whether USD 5,000 in mental distress is recoverable, and whether the judgment may be provisionally enforced? [Para 40][Para 41]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Bank D, creditor):** the credit and mortgage contracts of 25 June 2018 are written proof of a USD 34,000 loan; the borrowers paid two months and then deliberately evaded the debt despite repeated written demands, so principal, contractual interest, penalty rates and mental distress all fall due. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 22]
**Defendants (Ms C1, Mr C2, Mr C3, borrowers):** Ms C1 admitted the principal debt of USD 32,566.66 but asked the court to reject everything else — the ordinary and overdue interest, the 15% post-maturity rate, the 25% breach rate and the USD 5,000 distress claim — saying they had no means to pay; Mr C2 and Mr C3 did not appear and filed no defence. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 33]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the three defendants must pay USD 32,566.66 principal, USD 1,954.48 in ordinary and overdue interest, post-maturity interest at 15% a year from 26 April 2019 until paid, damages at 25% a year of the sum owed from default until paid, and 5,000,000 (five million) riel in mental distress; the claim above that is dismissed; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
The court found the loan, the mortgage and the transfer letter proved, the debt admitted by Ms C1 and undisputed, and fixed the balance after crediting the two instalments paid. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] It held the agreed 10% a year rate lawful under Articles 584 and 585 of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 128 of 21 December 2011 on capped interest rates, so ordinary interest was awarded as claimed. [Para 39] The 15% post-maturity rate and the 25% "damages" — which the court characterised as interest in another form — exceeded the 18% annual ceiling, and the court therefore fixed the rate at 18% a year from 26 April 2019. [Para 39] The distress claim was assessed on the actual facts and converted to riel. [Para 40][Para 47] Provisional enforcement followed from the admitted principal under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, with costs under Article 64. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where parties agree to post-maturity interest or to "damages" calculated as a percentage of the outstanding debt, the court treats both as interest and enforces them only up to the 18% annual ceiling set by Prakas No. 128, the contractual principal and ordinary interest being recoverable in full once admitted. [Para 39][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** the court's remarks that a documented, admitted debt in writing grounds provisional enforcement under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, and that this judgment is a factual decision and not a model precedent. [Para 1][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The court's reasoning caps the overdue rate at 18% a year, yet the operative order repeats the claimed 15% post-maturity rate and the 25% breach rate verbatim, alongside a dismissal of "the claim above this". [Para 39][Para 45][Para 46][Para 48] The mortgage and transfer letter are dated 20 April 2018 in the facts and in the court's findings but 20 February 2018 in the recital of the plaintiff's statement. [Para 14][Para 18][Para 35] The 15% and 25% figures are elsewhere expressed as a "25% interest rate" for breach damages, leaving it unclear whether they are cumulative. [Para 10][Para 46] Two defendants (Mr C2 and Mr C3) were absent without excuse and the judgment does not record any default procedure against them. [Para 23] Names survive only as initials (Bank D, Mr T D, Ms C1, Mr C2, Mr C3) and the judgment number, contract numbers and property title numbers are left blank in the OCR. [Para 2][Para 26][Para 28]
