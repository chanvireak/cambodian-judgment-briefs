# Company D v Company (name redacted) (Civil Judgment No. 1502 "ត", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 26 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ros Piseth) · first instance · Doc ID 143280

## 1. Facts
The plaintiff, a company registered in Malaysia, printed annual directories and other publications for the defendant company from 2012 on the defendant's instructions. [Para 8][Para 58] By 2015 the defendant had run up unpaid printing charges on seven invoices totalling USD 53,743.80 and had failed to pay a further invoice of 17 October 2016 for the 2017 directory in the sum of USD 39,200, due 01 December 2016. [Para 9][Para 16] After paying USD 29,788.83 across seven instalments the defendant still owed USD 23,955.97 on the first batch, and nothing on the second. [Para 14][Para 17]
The plaintiff sued for USD 75,955.98 including interest to 01 October 2017, 1.5% monthly interest thereafter, USD 10,000 in legal fees and costs. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6] The defendant company was absent at every stage and filed no defence. [Para 40]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a creditor proves its debt where every document relied on — invoices, delivery notes, item lists, exchange-rate tables — was generated by the creditor itself and acknowledged by no one on the debtor's side? [Para 61][Para 63]
2. Whether contractual interest at 1.5% a month and legal fees can be awarded when the principal sum itself has not been established? [Para 62]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Company D, creditor):** the defendant ordered directories year after year and received them; its own invoices, delivery notes and demand letters establish the services, the amounts and the default, so Articles 389, 390, 319 and 399 of the Civil Code entitle it to the principal, default interest, legal costs and costs of suit. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 20][Para 21]
**Defendant (company, debtor):** no defence was filed and no representative appeared, so the court decided on the plaintiff's evidence alone; the judgment records the defendant's silence rather than any contention. [Para 40]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is dismissed in its entirety and costs of suit are borne by the plaintiff. [Para 66][Para 67]
The court accepted the claim was properly filed under Article 75 of the Code of Civil Procedure but then examined the documents one by one. [Para 57] The invoices, statements and demand letters were all prepared by the plaintiff itself and carried no acknowledgment by the defendant, so they lacked the quality needed for judicial consideration. [Para 61] The delivery notes, invoices, item lists and exchange-rate tables did show that orders were placed, but they stated no prices and could not establish the size of the debt; and the specimen signatures and seals could not be attributed to the defendant company or its representative, so no admission of the documents' accuracy could be inferred. [Para 63][Para 64] Because the principal was not quantified, the court held it could not reach the interest or the distress element at all. [Para 62] On that basis the whole claim failed for want of sufficient evidence. [Para 65]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A creditor who sues on a running account bears the burden of producing evidence of the debt that is either acknowledged by the debtor or independently verifiable; documents created unilaterally by the creditor, unsupported by proof of price or of the signature and seal of the debtor, are insufficient, and accessory claims for interest and costs fall with the unproven principal. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the court's statement that it could not consider interest or mental distress because the principal had not yet been quantified, and its observation that the claim was nonetheless procedurally regular under Article 75. [Para 57][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The judgment twice describes the claim as covering "interest and mental distress", yet the pleaded claims contain no distress head — only 1.5% monthly interest, USD 10,000 legal fees and costs. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 62] The demand letter is dated 10 October 2017 in the narrative and in the evidence list but 12 October 2017 where the court summarises the claim. [Para 19][Para 54][Para 60] Paragraph 4 states the USD 75,955.98 covers 2015 and 2016 directory services, while paragraph 9 attributes USD 39,200 of it to the 2017 directory. [Para 4][Para 9] The section headed "disputed issues" records "none" although the claim was fully contested on the evidence and dismissed. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 65] Party and company names survive only as initials (Company D, Mr T D, Ms T C) and invoice, contract and title numbers are blank in the OCR. [Para 1][Para 3]
