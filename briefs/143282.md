# Bank C v Ms (name redacted) (Civil Judgment No. 821 "ជ១", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 1 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ke Manera, deputy president) · first instance · Doc ID 143282

## 1. Facts
Under a credit letter of 25 August 2016 and a loan contract of 21 September 2016, Bank C lent the defendant USD 90,000 over 15 years at 7.99% a year for the purchase of a home, secured by a mortgage over one property holding a private-ownership certificate of 27 May 2014; the contract fixed penalty interest at 27% a year for breach. [Para 11][Para 19][Para 20][Para 24] After repeated late payments the bank raised the rate to 9% from 01 January 2018 and then to 11% from 01 July 2018 by written notices, and the defendant stopped paying altogether from 01 July 2018, leaving USD 84,852.30 of principal. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] The defendant never appeared at any stage despite service at her residence and public notification. [Para 17][Para 28]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the court may hear the case and give judgment in the defendant's absence once service and public notification have been duly effected? [Para 28]
2. Whether the agreed penalty interest of 27% a year is recoverable on the outstanding principal? [Para 38][Para 39]
3. Whether USD 10,000 claimed as lost time, expenses and lawyers' fees is recoverable where its quantum is not proved? [Para 41][Para 42]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Bank C, creditor):** the credit letter, loan contract, mortgage deed and three reminder letters prove the USD 90,000 loan, the escalations to 9% and then 11%, and the default from 01 July 2018; the contract's penalty clause entitles it to 27% a year on the unpaid sums, plus USD 10,000 for lost time and legal service. [Para 9][Para 12][Para 13][Para 33]
**Defendant (borrower, name redacted):** she entered no appearance and filed no statement at any stage, so no contention was advanced and the court proceeded on the plaintiff's documents alone. [Para 17][Para 28]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendant must pay USD 84,852.30 principal, interest at 11% a year from 01 July 2018 until paid, and penalty interest at 27% a year on the unpaid sum from the same date; the claim for lost time, expenses and USD 10,000 in legal fees is dismissed; costs lie on the defendant; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal alone. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
The court first held that it could adjudicate in the defendant's absence under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure, having served her properly and published the summons, and recorded the Article 4 duty to conduct civil litigation fairly and promptly. [Para 28][Para 29] On the merits it found the credit letter proved the purchase of the property and the loan contract proved the borrowing, so each party was bound by the terms agreed. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] The reminder letters of 21 November 2017, 02 August 2018 and 05 September 2018, together with the lawyer's notice of 08 February 2018, established a serious breach in the non-payment of instalments. [Para 33][Para 35] The penalty was upheld because both parties had agreed it in section 1.6(a) of the home-loan letter and point 2(3) of the loan contract. [Para 39][Para 40] The USD 10,000 claim failed: the extent of loss was unproven, and the defendant was already penalised for the breach, so no further sum was warranted. [Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A court may enter judgment against an absent defendant once service and public notification are complete under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure, and it will enforce a penalty clause that both parties signed in the loan documents; but a claim for legal fees and general expenses fails where the claimant proves no quantum and the penalty already sanctions the breach. [Para 28][Para 39][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that litigants must conduct civil proceedings honestly and sincerely under Article 4, and that provisional enforcement extends to the principal only. [Para 29][Para 50]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The notice raising interest to 11% is dated 06 June 2018 in the narrative but 06 June 2017 in the evidence list, which would place it before the 9% increase it follows. [Para 13][Para 23] The case number appears as 2307 filed 05 November 2017 on the cover but as "2307/05-11-2018" throughout the body, a year later. [Para 1][Para 6][Para 46] The bank is styled "Bank C" in the narrative and with an Arabic character ("ធនាគារ ب") on the cover, and the defendant's name, identity document and address are wholly redacted. [Para 1][Para 2] The property certificate date is given as 27 May 2014 in the evidence list and as "May 2014" in the recital of the plaintiff's statement. [Para 11][Para 24]
