# Ms D v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1546 "ត", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 5 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ros Piseth) · first instance · Doc ID 143285

## 1. Facts
On 30 December 2008 Ms D lent Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2 USD 6,000 for six months, from 30 December 2008 to 30 July 2009, at USD 150 a month, taking house and rice-field plans certified by the commune chief as security. [Para 10][Para 12][Para 63] The borrowers never paid a single instalment of interest or principal; Ms D pursued them by telephone and in person, and their promises to repay were repeatedly broken. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 54]
On 12 September 2018 she applied to the commune for reconciliation; on 22 October 2018 the commune reconciliation committee recorded that both borrowers acknowledged the USD 6,000 debt and agreed to hand over a 25 m by 50 m plot in settlement, then refused to identify the land. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 62] She filed in court on 12 November 2018, claiming the principal, USD 150 a month from 30 January 2009, USD 20,000 for lost time, distress and legal service, costs and provisional enforcement. [Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the claim on a 2008 loan, brought in November 2018, is extinguished by limitation under Articles 480 to 482 of the Civil Code? [Para 11][Para 33]
2. Whether the borrowers' acknowledgment of the debt in the commune reconciliation record of 22 October 2018 stopped time running and started a fresh limitation period? [Para 59][Para 62]
3. Whether interest of USD 150 a month is enforceable, or must be reduced to the statutory rate under Article 318? [Para 65]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, creditor):** the contract of 30 December 2008 is in writing and untouched by any payment; the borrowers acknowledged the debt before the commune on 22 October 2018 and promised to settle it in land, so time never ran against her and she is entitled to principal, monthly interest, damages and costs. [Para 11][Para 36][Para 43]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2, borrowers):** the loan dates from 30 December 2008 and the claim came more than ten years later, so the right of action is time-barred under Articles 480 to 482; the commune reconciliation record is unlawful, and the petition should be struck out under Article 81 of the Code of Civil Procedure with costs against the plaintiff. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 26][Para 34]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants must repay USD 6,000 with interest at 5% a year from 30 January 2009 until paid; the USD 20,000 claim for lost time, distress and legal service is dismissed; costs lie on the defendants; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]
The court first noted that more than ten years had passed since the contract and that on its face the claim would be out of time under Articles 480 to 482. [Para 26][Para 33][Para 61] It then held that the reconciliation record of 22 October 2018 proved the borrowers had acknowledged the existence of their obligation, which rendered the expired period meaningless and started a new limitation period running from the date of that acknowledgment. [Para 62] On that footing the debt survived and the principal was ordered repaid. [Para 63][Para 64] The agreed USD 150 a month was found to exceed the interest allowed by law, so the court awarded interest at 5% a year under Article 318 instead. [Para 65] The USD 20,000 head failed on two grounds: no reputational, physical or psychological injury was shown or evidenced, and lawyers' fees are recoverable only where the parties could have foreseen them when contracting, which they did not. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A debtor's acknowledgment of the obligation — including before a commune reconciliation committee — nullifies the limitation period already run and starts a fresh one from the date of acknowledgment, so a stale claim revives; but agreed interest above the statutory rate is replaced by the 5% annual rate of Article 318, and lawyers' fees are not recoverable unless they were foreseeable at the time of contracting. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the court's observations that the borrowers' repeated promises to pay explained the creditor's delay without affecting the limitation analysis, and that an acknowledgment may be inferred from conduct as well as from a written record. [Para 39][Para 43][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The loan runs six months from 30 December 2008 to 30 July 2009, yet interest is claimed and awarded from 30 January 2009, a date the judgment never explains. [Para 10][Para 6][Para 72] The limitation provisions are cited inconsistently — Articles 480, 481 and 482 throughout, then "Article 489, point (b)/(c)" in the passage holding that acknowledgment restarted time — and the plaintiff speaks of a five-year period where the court measures ten. [Para 33][Para 38][Para 62] Paragraphs 24 to 25 of the OCR disclose the full names of the creditor and both borrowers, which are redacted as initials everywhere else, and give the wife's name in three different forms across paragraphs 24, 32 and 25. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 32] The property descriptions in paragraph 12 carry certificate and plot numbers that the OCR has dropped, and the security is described as unregistered commune plans rather than a registered mortgage. [Para 12]
