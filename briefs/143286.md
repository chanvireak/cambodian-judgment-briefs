# Mr D v Ms C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1540 "S", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 2 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Cham Chan Piseth) · first instance · Doc ID 143286

## 1. Facts
Mr D says he bought 5,752 m² of land at Kep in 1990 and holds a certificate of the right to possess and use land (ប័ណ្ណសំគាល់សិទ្ធិកាន់កាប់ប្រើប្រាស់ដីធ្លី) dated 14 March 1992, leaving the plot vacant and visiting it occasionally. [Para 11] When the area was registered under LMAP in 2013–2014 he was handed certificates whose total area was short by 1,517 m²: four certificates now sit on the plot, two in his name and two in the defendants' — 919 m² to Mr C2 and 598 m² to Ms C1 — and fencing had been built along the defendants' plans. [Para 11][Para 12]
He sued on 23 February 2016 (complaint registered 22 March 2016) to restrain any dealing with the land and recover it. [Para 22] The 2014 certificates were issued in September 2014 after a 30-day public publication to which no one objected. [Para 21][Para 20]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the land covered by the 2014 certificates belongs to the plaintiff or to the defendants? [Para 34]
2. Whether a certificate issued over land already held under an earlier possession certificate, where the registry notation does not match the boundaries recorded in the parent certificate, transfers ownership to the later holder? [Para 42][Para 43]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, original owner):** he bought the 5,752 m² plot in 1990, was issued the possession certificate of 14 March 1992, and held it peacefully for 24 years without dispute from any neighbour, satisfying Articles 30, 31 and 38 of the Land Law; the defendants' titles descend from a certificate dated 28 March 1992 — later than his own — and he asks that his claim be preserved as pleaded. [Para 12][Para 23]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2):** Ms C1 bought 598 m² from name 12 in 1992 for 43 chi of gold (៤៣ ជីមាស), paying a USD 150 deposit on a private deed with no witness or authority present, and has occupied it since; Mr C2 bought through a sale contract of 14 October 2005 with stamp duty of 160,000,000 riel paid; registration was published for 30 days and Mr D did not object, so under Sub-Decree No. 46 ANKr.BK of 31 May 2002 and Article 40 of the Land Law he is time-barred from complaining; they claim USD 50,000 for lost time and mental distress and dismissal of the claim. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 24][Para 31]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the land held under the 2014 certificates is returned to Mr D; Mr C2 and Ms C1 are ordered to carry out the registration formalities in Mr D's name; Ms C1's counterclaim is dismissed; costs lie on the defendants under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 49]
The court compared the two chains of title. Mr D's possession certificate of 14 March 1992 describes a 5,752 m² plot bounded north by the national road, south by the path to the sea mouth, east by the Soriya Hotel path and west by the path down to the sea mouth; name 7's certificate of 28 March 1992 covers 1,867 m² bounded north by plot 14, south by plot 15, east by the stream (អូរ) and west by plots 16 and 17. [Para 36][Para 37] Because the earlier certificate is earlier in date and the recorded boundaries do not coincide, the two plots never adjoined, and the survey record of 10 March 1992 confirms Mr D's plot was a stand-alone lot surrounded by roads. [Para 38][Para 39]
Mr C2's certificate of 4 November 2005 (1,026 m²) descends from name 7's 1992 certificate, but the boundaries written into the 2005 certificate differ from those in the parent certificate, and no evidence showed that any adjoining owner had changed — so the descent recorded by the land office did not establish a true pedigree. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] The 598 m² certificate issued to Ms C1 on 18 September 2014 recites a western boundary against Mr D's plot although the land she says she bought from name 12 lay against the stream; her 1992 private sale letter specified neither location nor boundaries and was unwitnessed, so it could not tie her purchase to the titled land. That certificate was issued by mistake and encroaches on Mr D's plot. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The counterclaim for USD 50,000 rested on no evidence. [Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a later certificate is carved out of an earlier possession certificate, the boundary descriptions in the two documents differ, and the land office's transfer notation is contradicted by those descriptions with no proof that the adjoining owners changed, the later registration is an error and cannot defeat the earlier-dated title; the earlier certificate holder keeps the land. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** none — the defendants' LMAP publication and time-bar argument was recorded but not ruled on. [Para 24]
