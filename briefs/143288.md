# Bank (Oddar Meanchey branch) v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Judgment No. 14 "K", Oddar Meanchey Court of First Instance, 22 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ly Chantha) · first instance · Doc ID 143288

## 1. Facts
On 21 November 2016 Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2 signed a small-scale credit contract (ឥណទានខ្នាតតូច) with the bank's Oddar Meanchey branch for USD 20,000 (twenty thousand), repayable by monthly instalments over 60 months with full repayment due on 16 December 2021, at 1.30% interest a month and a penalty rate of 24% a year on sums unpaid. [Para 9][Para 20][Para 25] A hypothec contract of the same date secured the loan. [Para 27]
They paid 13 instalments up to 5 March 2019 — USD 4,333.29 (four thousand three hundred and thirty-three dollars and twenty-nine cents) of principal and USD 3,199.59 (three thousand one hundred and ninety-nine dollars and fifty-nine cents) of interest — then stopped, leaving USD 15,666.71 (fifteen thousand six hundred and sixty-six dollars and seventy-one cents) of principal outstanding, unpaid in principal or interest since 6 February 2018. [Para 11][Para 21][Para 22] The bank sued for the balance, 1.30% monthly interest from 6 February 2018, the 24% penalty from 9 February 2018, 10,000,000 (ten million) riel for legal fees, distress and lost time, provisional enforcement and costs. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a defendant who was lawfully summoned, served at his home through his child, and still fails to appear may be treated as having confessed the facts alleged and judgment given against him in absentia? [Para 24][Para 35]
2. Whether a penalty of 24% a year on overdue sums is enforceable in addition to 1.30% monthly interest, where the borrowers agreed to both in the contract? [Para 30][Para 31]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank, represented by Mr T1D and Mr T2D):** it produced the credit contract of 21 November 2016, the hypothec contracts of 15 March 2012 and 21 November 2016 and the repayment schedule of 23 November 2016; USD 20,000 was disbursed, 13 instalments were paid, and USD 15,666.71 of principal remains due with contractual interest and penalty; it also asks for 10,000,000 riel of costs, distress and lost time, provisional enforcement and costs. [Para 13][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2, borrowers):** they entered no appearance and filed no statement, though the summons was served on them at their residence and, they being absent, handed to their natural child living with them; they contest nothing. [Para 24][Para 35]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the borrowers must pay the bank USD 15,666.71 of principal, 1.30% monthly interest on that principal from 6 February 2018 until full payment, and the 24% annual penalty on the overdue sums from 9 February 2018 until payment; the claim for 10,000,000 riel is allowed at 6,000,000 (six million) riel; item 1(a) of the order — the principal — is provisionally enforceable; costs are borne by the defendants. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]
The court first dealt with the absent defendants: the summons had been delivered at their home, no one was there, and it was given to their child who lived with them, yet they did not appear at the fixed date, so the court tried the case in their absence and treated them as having confessed the facts alleged by the bank. [Para 35] On the evidence it found that the defendants had in fact borrowed USD 20,000 from the bank, had repaid part, and still owed USD 15,666.71, so the principal claim was granted. [Para 29]
Interest at 1.30% a month was upheld as consistent with the parties' written agreement of 21 November 2016 and lawful. [Para 30] The penalty at 24% a year was also upheld, apportioned to the number of days of default from 9 February 2018, on the ground that the borrowers had expressly accepted the penalty clause in the contract, so the court gave effect to the parties' agreement. [Para 31] On the 10,000,000 riel claim the court accepted that the default had genuinely harmed the bank — legal consultation, lost time and travel to court — but found the sum excessive, since the contract already gave the bank a penalty rate as the price of that harm, and fixed 6,000,000 riel on the actual damage and the defendants' means. [Para 32][Para 39] Provisional enforcement was granted under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure but confined to the principal, as the priority matter. [Para 33][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A defendant who, after lawful service at his residence, fails to appear without excuse is taken to have confessed the facts pleaded, and the court may adjudicate in absentia on the documentary evidence alone; a penalty clause the borrower expressly accepted is enforced as written, but damages claimed on top of it are reduced to the actual harm proved. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 35]

**Obiter dicta:** provisional enforcement under Article 196 should be limited to the principal, because it is the priority matter, and granting it more widely would prejudice both sides. [Para 33]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The bank's name survives only as "the bank", with the branch given and the signatories as Mr T1D and Mr T2D; the defendants appear only as C1 and C2. [Para 1][Para 2] One hypothec contract produced is dated 15 March 2012, four years before the loan it is said to secure, and the judgment never explains it. [Para 26] Interest runs from 6 February 2018 but the penalty from 9 February 2018, with no reason given for the three-day gap. [Para 4][Para 5] Paragraphs 16 and 30 are stray fragments ("riel." / "the bank") and the party names are scrambled in paragraphs 19, 27 and 36. [Para 16][Para 19][Para 30] The award falls from 10,000,000 to 6,000,000 riel with no arithmetic shown. [Para 32][Para 39]
