# Ms D v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 03(K), Siem Reap Court of First Instance, 28 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Chi Sok, president of the court) · first instance · Doc ID 143289

## 1. Facts
On 10 July 2017 Ms D lent Mr C1 and Ms C2 USD 75,000 (seventy-five thousand) under a loan contract and a contract creating a hypothec (កិច្ចសន្យាបង្កើតសិទ្ធិហ៊ីប៉ូតែក) over a titled plot, at 5% interest a month, repayable on 10 July 2018. [Para 10][Para 11] The borrowers paid interest in instalments totalling USD 7,150 up to 8 March 2018, which exceeded the interest permitted by the statutory cap by USD 478.36; that excess was set off against principal, leaving USD 74,521.64 outstanding. [Para 20][Para 30] On 26 April 2018 they signed a further undertaking to pay USD 13,100 within three months, but paid nothing. [Para 31][Para 32]
Ms D sued on 10 August 2018 for the principal, USD 7,331.64 of interest, late interest at 27% a year from 11 July 2018, USD 20,000 in legal fees and distress, costs and provisional enforcement. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7] The defendants counterclaimed on 12 September 2018. [Para 42]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether interest the borrowers paid above the statutorily permitted rate must be credited against the principal rather than kept by the lender? [Para 30][Para 64]
2. Whether late-payment interest may be awarded at 27% a year — 1.5 times the capped 18% rate — where the contract stipulated 100% but the lender claimed only 27%? [Para 73][Para 78]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, lender):** the contract of 10 July 2017 and the receipt of the same date prove that USD 75,000 was handed over; the borrowers defaulted, ignored her notice to perform, and now owe USD 74,521.64 of principal with interest and 27% late interest, plus USD 20,000 for legal service, lost time and distress; the counterclaim has no factual basis at all. [Para 12][Para 34][Para 35][Para 44]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2, borrowers):** the ledger she wrote by hand on 10 July 2017 wrongly rolled up two earlier loans — USD 10,000 on 28 September 2016 and USD 5,000 on 28 January 2017, with advance deductions — plus the discharge of a microfinance debt, so the true balance is USD 46,883.57 principal and USD 3,508.58 interest to 28 June 2018; they ask the court to annul the contract and dismiss the rest. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the borrowers must pay USD 74,521.64 of principal, USD 7,331.64 of interest to 26 July 2018, late interest at 27% a year on the principal from 27 July 2018 until payment, and 10,000,000 (ten million) riel for legal service, distress and other damage; anything more is refused; costs and taxes lie on them; the order for principal is provisionally enforceable; the counterclaim is set aside in points 1–5 and dismissed in points 6–7. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 94]
The court accepted the loan contract and the receipt of 10 July 2017 as proving that USD 75,000 was actually handed over, and it held that the interest already paid had to be recalculated against the statutory cap: USD 7,150 was paid, USD 478.36 of it exceeded the permitted interest, and that surplus reduced the principal to USD 74,521.64. [Para 30][Para 67][Para 69] It rejected the borrowers' three-loan reconciliation outright, because those were earlier and different transactions that could not be added together and set off against this principal, and no credible evidence supported the figures. [Para 68]
On late interest the court reasoned from Article 399 of the Civil Code, that a debtor cannot escape interest for the period of delay, and from Article 2 of Prakas No. 128 K.Y. of 21 December 2011 capping agreed interest at 1.5 times the permitted rate: 1.5 × 18% = 27% a year. [Para 75][Para 78] Since the contract stipulated 100% and the lender claimed only 27%, the claim was within the cap and was granted. [Para 73][Para 79] Damages were awarded under Articles 398 and 401, assessed on the actual loss, the situation and the borrowers' means, and fixed at 10,000,000 riel against a claim of USD 20,000. [Para 80][Para 91] Provisional enforcement was confined to the principal, because Article 350 gives such an order the force of a final judgment and any wider order could be reversed on appeal to the prejudice of both sides. [Para 84][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a moneylender has charged interest above the statutory ceiling, the excess is not forfeited to the lender but is applied to reduce the principal, and late-payment interest is then capped at 1.5 times the permitted annual rate — 27% where the cap is 18% — even if the contract stipulated a far higher figure. [Para 30][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** a counterclaim that asks the court to annul a contract is misconceived, because annulment under Article 360 of the Civil Code operates by an expression of will for incapacity or defect of consent under Articles 14 and 345, not by judicial decree. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 86]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The late-interest start date is pleaded as 11 July 2018 but ordered as 27 July 2018, and paragraph 48 speaks of a repayment deadline of 26 July 2018 against a contract date of 10 July 2018. [Para 6][Para 48][Para 73][Para 90] The interest figure USD 7,331.64 is printed with words reading "seventy-four thousand five hundred and twenty-one", and the judgment later calls the same sum "seven thousand three hundred and thirty-one". [Para 5][Para 72][Para 89] The contract is dated 10 July 2019 in one finding where every other reference is 2017. [Para 67] Interest is described as calculated at 3%, at 1.5% and at 5% a month within the same reasoning. [Para 32][Para 71] The sum USD 13,100 is unexplained against a principal of USD 74,521.64. [Para 31][Para 70] Borrower names survive only as C1 and C2. [Para 3]
