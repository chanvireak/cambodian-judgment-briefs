# Mr D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 1505 "B", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 27 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Bun Thy) · first instance · Doc ID 143291

## 1. Facts
On 30 December 2013 Ms C borrowed USD 50,000 (fifty thousand) from Mr D, a Japanese national, at 3% interest a month; she agreed that three months' interest, USD 4,500 (four thousand five hundred), be deducted in advance, so she actually received USD 45,500 (forty-five thousand five hundred), as recorded in a Japanese-language receipt and an ACLEDA bank transfer slip of the same date, and she promised to repay within six months. [Para 5][Para 9][Para 10][Para 33] She paid neither interest nor principal thereafter, saying she had on-lent the money to a third party who could not repay. [Para 6][Para 11]
The plaintiff demanded payment by email and notices three times during 2016 and 2017, sued on 25 March 2019, and a criminal complaint he filed was shelved by the prosecutor in favour of the civil route. [Para 14][Para 31][Para 48]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether 3% monthly interest can be recovered where the agreement creating the right to interest was not set out in a written instrument signed by the borrower? [Para 53]
2. Whether the claim is time-barred under the five-year period in Article 482 of the Civil Code where the creditor gave demand notices within that period? [Para 26][Para 48][Para 49]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, lender):** Ms C wanted to on-lend money at interest, borrowed USD 50,000 at 3% a month, accepted the USD 4,500 advance deduction and received USD 45,500, promised repayment within six months, and paid nothing; he did not abandon his claim — he emailed repeatedly and sent notices, so no limitation period has run; the account the money was wired to belonged to her. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]
**Defendants (Ms C, borrower):** she denies borrowing from the plaintiff at all, and says he never lent through her; she admits receiving around USD 40,000 from him, but that sum was money he was to hand to a male borrower, because as a Japanese national working for a Japanese employer he could not himself run a money-lending business; she was merely a name through which the plaintiff dealt with that borrower, the emails referred to that man, and in any event the loan dates from 30 March 2013 with a three-month term, so more than six years have passed and the claim is time-barred under Article 482. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 29][Para 42]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Ms C must pay USD 45,500 (forty-five thousand five hundred) plus 12,000,000 (twelve million) riel for lost time, distress and legal fees; the claims for 3% monthly interest and for late interest are dismissed; point 1 is provisionally enforceable; costs lie on the defendant. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]
The court first resolved the date: the receipt and the bank slip are both dated 30 December 2013, and it held the parties did in fact form a loan contract on that date under Article 579 of the Civil Code, the defendant admitting she received USD 45,500 from the plaintiff. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] It rejected her account that she was only a conduit for another borrower: the earlier judgment of 27 April 2015 of the same court records that she had herself lent USD 50,000 at 3% a month to that man, which is a separate transaction from this one, and she produced no witness to prove otherwise. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
On the rate, the court held that although the agreement stipulated 3% a month, the agreement creating the right to that interest was not made in a written instrument signed by the borrower, so the claim to interest had no effect under Article 583 (3) of the Civil Code and was dismissed. [Para 53] The claim to late-payment interest was dismissed too, because the parties had not agreed one and no provision of law provides for it. [Para 54] Because three months' interest had been deducted in advance, the sum actually handed over — USD 45,500 — was taken as the principal under Article 585 (5). [Para 52] On limitation, the court found the plaintiff had given notice of his demand three times in 2016 and 2017, so the defendant's plea that the five-year period under Article 482 had expired was wrong. [Para 48][Para 49] Damages were assessed on the actual situation under Article 400 and fixed at 12,000,000 riel against a claim of USD 20,000. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where money is advanced under an oral or partly written loan at a monthly rate but the agreement creating the right to interest is not embodied in a written instrument signed by the borrower, the interest is irrecoverable and the principal is the sum actually delivered net of any advance deduction of interest. [Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** a debtor's plea that a money claim is time-barred fails where the creditor can show demand notices given within the five-year period, since those interrupt the period. [Para 48][Para 49]
