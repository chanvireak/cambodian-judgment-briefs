# Bank (Kampong Cham branch) v Ms C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1501 "T", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 26 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ros Piseth) · first instance · Doc ID 143292

## 1. Facts
On 17 March 2016 the defendants signed a short-term credit contract with the bank for USD 300,000 (three hundred thousand) at 9% a year over twelve months, with 150% of that rate — 13.5% a year — as default interest on late payment, secured by two hypothecs of 7 November and 28 December 2016 over titled land. [Para 12][Para 20][Para 46][Para 47] They paid only interest, stopped on 11 January 2017 after repaying USD 60,000 of principal on 10 January 2017, and the bank sued on 27 August 2018 for the remaining USD 240,000, USD 22,770 of interest to 31 December 2017, 13.5% a year thereafter, USD 50,000 for distress, costs, provisional enforcement and a declaration of priority ranking. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 11][Para 13][Para 14]
The defendants entered no appearance at any stage but filed a written statement through counsel on 26 December 2018. [Para 27]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a bank must first obtain rescission of the credit contract before it can sue for the outstanding principal and interest? [Para 28][Para 63]
2. Whether a bank, being a legal person, may recover damages for mental distress in addition to the contractual default interest? [Para 42][Para 82]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank, through counsel appearing for it):** USD 300,000 was lent at 9% a year for twelve months against two hypothecs; USD 60,000 of principal was repaid on 10 January 2017 and nothing since, leaving USD 240,000; the interest claimed to 31 December 2017 is USD 22,770 computed from the bank's own repayment system, after which the contract's 13.5% default rate runs; it also claims USD 50,000 for distress, provisional enforcement, costs, and a declaration of the existence of its preferential right (ប្រាតិភោគ) to be paid in priority. [Para 11][Para 14][Para 17][Para 19][Para 25]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2, borrowers):** the bank cannot claim principal, interest, penalty and damages without first seeking rescission of the credit contract, because Article 411(3) of the Civil Code requires rescission before restitution; the outstanding principal is USD 200,000 not USD 240,000, because they sold a house and the bank set USD 100,000 of the price against the debt; the interest computation is wrong, since 9% on USD 200,000 is only USD 18,000; and the extra 4.5% is a penalty that cannot run as interest. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 31][Para 34][Para 36]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants must pay USD 240,000 of principal, USD 22,770 of interest to 31 December 2017, and 13.5% a year on the principal from 1 January 2018 until full payment; the claim for USD 50,000 of distress and the claim to declare a priority ranking are set aside; point 1.1 (the principal) is provisionally enforceable; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]
On the principal the court preferred the bank's account statements for the defendant's account from 17 March 2016 to 1 December 2018, with the statement of interest and principal of 3 January 2019, showing USD 240,000 outstanding; the defendants admitted the contract and the default but produced no evidence to displace that figure, so their USD 200,000 version failed. [Para 59][Para 60]
On the rescission argument the court held it had no legal basis: rescission may be effected immediately by an expression of will communicated to the other party, and may be done outside court under Articles 407 and 409 of the Civil Code, so a claim for the amounts due is a proper claim in itself. [Para 63] The 9% rate was held to fall within the statutory ceiling, by reference to Article 583 of the Civil Code, Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code and Article 1 of Prakas No. 128 K.Y. of 21 December 2011, so the interest of USD 22,770 to 31 December 2017 was awarded. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] The 13.5% default rate was upheld as late-payment interest under Article 399, calculated at the rate the parties had agreed in advance in the contract. [Para 74][Para 75] The court expressly rejected the argument that a penalty cannot run like interest, holding that parties may agree on late-payment interest as pre-agreed compensation for the debtor's fault. [Para 78][Para 79]
The distress claim failed twice over: the plaintiff is a legal person, which cannot suffer mental distress, and its property loss is already made good by default interest. [Para 42][Para 82][Para 83] The priority-ranking claim was refused because the hypothec already exists. [Para 25]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A creditor need not obtain a court judgment rescinding a credit contract before suing for the sums due, because rescission may be effected out of court by an expression of will; and a corporate lender cannot recover damages for mental distress, its loss being fully repaired by the agreed default interest. [Para 63][Para 82][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** a contractual penalty may validly be framed as late-payment interest running at an agreed rate on the principal, since the parties are free to fix compensation in advance. [Para 78][Para 79]
