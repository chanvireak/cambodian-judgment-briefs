# Bank (Kandal branch) v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1553 "D", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 6 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Im Vanna) · first instance · Doc ID 143293

## 1. Facts
On 27 March 2018 Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2 borrowed USD 93,000.00 (ninety-three thousand) from the bank under a loan contract for 120 months at 10% a year, repayable at USD 1,239.44 a month under a schedule dated 29 March 2018 running from 7 May 2018 to 29 March 2028, secured by a hypothec of 28 March 2018 over a titled property. [Para 9][Para 15][Para 32] They paid 278 days, about nine months, totalling USD 11,891.76 (eleven thousand eight hundred and ninety-one dollars and fifty-six cents), of which USD 5,116.03 was principal and USD 6,775.73 interest, then stopped from 6 February 2019. [Para 10][Para 11]
The bank sued on 3 July 2019 for USD 89,054.89 of principal, 10% interest from 5 February 2019, a 25% penalty from 6 February 2019 and USD 8,500 in legal fees, lost time and expenses. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 34] After the claim was filed the defendants paid another USD 11,891.76 on 29 July 2019, which the bank applied to principal and interest down to 10 November 2019, leaving USD 83,938.86 (eighty-three thousand nine hundred and thirty-eight dollars and eighty-six cents). [Para 13][Para 24]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a bank may recover the whole outstanding balance, and late interest, where the contract was to run until 2028 and the borrower defaulted in February 2019? [Para 36][Para 37]
2. Whether the agreed 25% annual penalty is lawful, and whether legal fees, lost time and other expenses may be added on top of contractual interest and penalty? [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank, represented by Mr Ta D and counsel Eang Sour Sen):** USD 93,000 was lent on 27 March 2018 for 120 months at 10% a year with a 25% penalty for breach, secured by hypothec over a titled plot; the borrowers paid nine months and defaulted from 6 February 2019; USD 89,054.89 of principal was due at filing, and it claims 10% interest from 5 February 2019, the 25% penalty from 6 February 2019, USD 8,500 for legal service, lost time and procedural expenses, and provisional enforcement. [Para 4][Para 7][Para 8][Para 14][Para 16]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2, borrowers):** they admit borrowing USD 93,000 on the terms pleaded, paying monthly until February 2019, the default being caused by financial difficulty, and the balance of USD 83,938.86 after the 29 July 2019 payment; they accept the 25% penalty and ask the court to declare the judgment provisionally enforceable, but contest the interest, penalty, legal fees and expenses on the ground that they have never had any problem with the bank. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 25]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants must pay USD 83,938.86 plus 25% a year on that sum from 11 November 2019 until full payment; everything else claimed is dismissed; point 1.1 is provisionally enforceable; costs lie on the defendants as the losing party. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 41]
The parties agreed on the essentials: the contract of 27 March 2018, the USD 93,000, the 10% rate, the 120-month term, the default from 6 February 2019 and the post-filing payment leaving USD 83,938.86. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] The court found the contract was due to end on 29 March 2028, but because the borrowers failed to perform the bank was entitled to claim principal, interest and the penalty under clause 11 of the annex; the creditor thereby gave up the remaining term as at the filing date of 3 July 2019. [Para 36] Ordinary interest was to be computed from the default to the filing date under Article 583, but since the defendants had already paid principal and interest to 10 November 2019, that obligation was satisfied, and only late interest remained, from 11 November 2019 under Articles 391 and 399. [Para 37]
The 25% penalty was upheld: the court treated it as late-payment interest governed by Articles 391 and 399, and held the rate lawful under Article 584 of the Civil Code and Article 2 of Prakas No. 128 K.Y. of 21 December 2011 of the Ministry of Justice on capped interest rates. [Para 38] The USD 8,500 claim failed on two grounds — the creditor already recovered principal, interest and the late-payment penalty, and it produced no specific figure for the lawyer's fee. [Para 39][Para 40] Provisional enforcement was granted for the principal only, as a money claim, under Article 196. [Para 42][Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A creditor may accelerate a long-term loan and sue for the whole balance on default, thereby waiving the unexpired term, but it recovers only the principal and the agreed late-payment rate; a further claim for legal fees and expenses fails where it duplicates the penalty already recovered or is unsupported by a specified amount. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** an agreed penalty rate is to be treated as late-payment interest and is lawful if within the ceiling set by Prakas No. 128 K.Y. [Para 38]
