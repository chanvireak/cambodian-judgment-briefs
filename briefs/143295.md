# Specialised Bank (name redacted) v Ms C1, Mr C2 and Mr C3 (Civil Judgment No. 825 K, Phnom Penh Municipal Court, 2 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Phi Manera, Vice-President) · first instance · Doc ID 143295

## 1. Facts
On 04 June 2018 Ms C1, Mr C2 and Mr C3 signed a master credit agreement with the plaintiff bank for 50,000$ (fifty thousand US dollars) at 1.2% (one point two per cent) interest per month and a 24% (twenty-four per cent) annual penalty on principal, secured by a mortgage over land and a house created by contract of 07 June 2018. [Para 18][Para 27] They paid for five months — 769$ (seven hundred sixty-nine US dollars) of principal and 3,215$ (three thousand two hundred fifteen US dollars) of interest — and stopped on 15 November 2018, leaving 49,231$ (forty-nine thousand two hundred thirty-one US dollars) outstanding. [Para 11][Para 19] The bank sued on 13 February 2019 for that balance, continuing interest and penalty from 16 November 2018, 10,000$ (ten thousand US dollars) for distress and loss of time, and costs. [Para 12][Para 15]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the co-defendants breached the credit agreement by ceasing payment, so that the outstanding principal with contractual interest and penalty is owed? [Para 29][Para 33]
2. Whether damages for distress and loss of time may be awarded on top of the contractual penalty where no evidence quantifies the loss? [Para 41]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank):** it disbursed the 50,000$ against a registered mortgage, the borrowers paid five instalments and then defaulted, ignoring repeated demands and lawyers' notices of 02, 16 and 17 January 2019; the agreement, the repayment schedule and the eight receipts prove the balance, so the court should order payment of principal, 1.2% monthly interest and the 24% annual penalty. [Para 12][Para 19][Para 26][Para 30] On the ancillary head it left the 10,000$ claim to the court's discretion. [Para 21]
**Defendants (Ms C1, Mr C2, Mr C3, borrowers):** they admit the loan, the rates and the outstanding 49,231$; they fell behind because of their own difficulties, are trying to sell the house and will pay in full once it sells, and ask the bank to waive the penalty and the 10,000$ damages claim because they cannot pay. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 25][Para 31]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the co-defendants are ordered to pay the bank 49,231$ principal, interest at 1.2% per month on that principal from 16 November 2018 until full payment, and a penalty of 24% per year on the principal from the same date until full payment; the claim for 10,000$ in damages for distress and loss of time is dismissed; costs lie on the co-defendants under Article 64 CPC, and the order for principal (point 1-K) is declared provisionally enforceable. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
The court began from Article 4 CPC — that courts must ensure civil actions proceed fairly and promptly and parties must act honestly and in good faith — then construed the credit agreement of 04 June 2018 and the mortgage contract of 07 June 2018 as binding the parties, who must perform what they agreed. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] It found the co-defendants had not performed: the lawyers' and bank's demand letters and the repayment schedule proved the default, and their own statements conceded both the debt and the failure. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] That made the breach serious and continuing, so principal, interest and the agreed penalty followed. [Para 33][Para 36][Para 38][Para 39] The 10,000$ damages claim failed because nothing evidenced the extent of the loss and because the bank already recovered the penalty, which the court took into account in refusing that head. [Para 41]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where borrowers admit a written credit agreement, a registered mortgage and a repayment schedule, and the lender's demand letters show arrears, the court will order the outstanding principal with the contractual monthly interest and the agreed default penalty from the date payments ceased; but a claim for distress and loss of time will be refused where no evidence establishes the amount of the loss and the lender already recovers a contractual penalty for the same breach. [Para 30][Para 38][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the invocation of Article 4 CPC, that civil proceedings must be conducted fairly and speedily and that the parties must act with honesty and good faith — stated as a general premise and not applied to any particular finding. [Para 26]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
All party names are redacted to initials — the bank as "ធនាគារ ឯកទេស" with its name reduced to "ដ", the borrowers as "ឈ្មោះ ច១", "ច២" and "ច៣" — and the first defendant is called "ឈ្មោះ ប១" in one passage. [Para 3][Para 27][Para 31] The parties' roles are scrambled in the recital of the agreement, which lists the bank among the lenders and the borrowers out of order. [Para 27] The security is described inconsistently: a transfer-of-possession letter dated 15 December 2015 — nearly three years before the 2018 loan — issued by the Khan governor is cited both as the mortgage instrument and alongside a separate mortgage contract of 07 June 2018. [Para 18][Para 26] Interest and penalty are pleaded as running "as at" (គិតត្រឹម) 16 November 2018 but awarded "from" (ចាប់ពី) that date, while the balance itself is computed to 15 November 2018. [Para 5][Para 11][Para 45]
