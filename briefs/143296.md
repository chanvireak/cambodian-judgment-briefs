# Microfinance Institution (name redacted) v Ms C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 20 K, Kampong Thom Provincial Court, 4 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Heng Chanthy Boren) · first instance · Doc ID 143296

## 1. Facts
On 16 March 2016 Ms C1 and her husband Mr C2 borrowed 10,000$ (ten thousand US dollars) from the plaintiff microfinance institution at 1.7% (one point seven per cent) per month over 36 months, repayable monthly under a schedule of 17 March 2016, and acknowledged receipt of the whole sum. [Para 9][Para 11][Para 12] They repaid part of the principal but ceased altogether from 05 June 2017, leaving 6,016$ (six thousand sixteen US dollars) outstanding, and have paid nothing since. [Para 10][Para 19] The institution sued for the balance, continuing interest, penalties of 0.1% a day on overdue principal and 0.2% a day on overdue interest, 8,000,000 riel in costs and lost time, and provisional enforcement. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a loan-for-consumption contract signed and thumbprinted by both parties is valid and enforceable under Articles 579 and 583 of the Civil Code, so as to bind the debtors to the outstanding principal? [Para 22]
2. Whether the contractual interest of 1.7% a month and the daily penalty clauses, admitted by the debtors, may be recognised, and on what basis damages are assessed? [Para 26][Para 27][Para 29]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the institution, through its representative Mr T):** the debtors signed the contract, received the full 10,000$, repaid only in part and have paid neither principal nor interest since 05 June 2017; the contract itself fixes the 1.7% monthly rate and the penalty rates, and the debtors admit them, so the claim should be granted in full. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 27]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2, debtors):** Ms C1's husband knew of the summons but was too busy to attend; she admits the contract, the borrowing of 10,000$, the remaining 6,016$ and the failure to pay interest since 05 June 2017, and asks to pay 200,000 (two hundred thousand) riel a month. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants must pay the institution 6,016$ principal; interest at 1.7% per month on that principal from 05 June 2017 until payment in full; penalties of 0.1% per day on overdue principal and 0.2% per day on overdue interest from the same date; and 2,000,000 (two million) riel in damages, the 8,000,000 riel claimed being cut down; point 1 of the order (the principal) is provisionally enforceable, and costs fall on the defendants under Article 64 CPC. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]
The court found the contract valid because it was made by agreement and bore the thumbprints of both parties, satisfying Articles 579 and 583 of the Civil Code, and because the debtors admitted receiving the full principal, the lapse of the repayment period and the 6,016$ balance. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] On interest it held the 1.7% monthly rate consistent with National Bank of Cambodia Prakas No. B7.09-213 of 09 September 2009 on liberalisation of interest-rate setting. [Para 26] The penalty clauses in clause 3 of the contract of 16 March 2016 were enforced because the debtors admitted them. [Para 27] Damages were available under Article 398 of the Civil Code for non-performance, but the court fixed them "according to the actual circumstances" rather than at the sum claimed, and granted provisional enforcement under Article 196 CPC to stop the running of interest injuring both sides. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A loan-for-consumption contract that both parties have signed and thumbprinted is valid under Articles 579 and 583 of the Civil Code, and where the debtors admit receipt of the principal and default, the court will enforce the outstanding balance with the contractual monthly interest — provided it is consistent with the National Bank of Cambodia's prakas on interest-rate setting — and the agreed daily penalties; damages under Article 398 are assessed on the actual circumstances and may be reduced below the sum claimed. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 27][Para 29]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that delay aggravates the debtors' interest burden and deprives the institution of the use of its money in its business, given as the reason for ordering provisional enforcement. [Para 30]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claim recital states the outstanding principal as "6.016 (ប្រាំបួន ពាន់ ដប់ ប្រាំមួយ)" — nine thousand sixteen — while every other passage, and the operative order, say 6,016$ (six thousand sixteen); the figures diverge by three thousand. [Para 3][Para 10][Para 33] The contract is dated 16 March 2016 in the narrative and the reasoning but 17 March 2016 in the summary and in the repayment schedule, and one reference gives 16 March 2016 for the penalty clause. [Para 9][Para 11][Para 21][Para 27] The operative paragraphs are badly scrambled by OCR, with the parties' names and the institution's name (reduced to "ដ") transposed across sub-paragraphs, and the daily interest penalty appears as "ការរបាក់" for "ការប្រាក់". [Para 33] The plaintiff is described as "ឈ្មោះ ត ដ", conflating the representative's name with the institution's redacted initial. [Para 1]
