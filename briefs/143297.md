# Microfinance Institution (name redacted) v Mr C1, Ms C2 and Ms C3 (Civil Judgment No. 13 K, Oddar Meanchey Provincial Court, 13 August 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Kol Kompul) · first instance · Doc ID 143297

## 1. Facts
On 03 November 2015 Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2 borrowed 25,000$ (twenty-five thousand US dollars) from the plaintiff's Anlong Veng branch at 1.6% (one point six per cent) per month over 52 months, with their daughter Ms C3 standing as guarantor, under a loan contract of 05 November 2015 and its annexed repayment schedule. [Para 10][Para 28] They paid principal in instalments but defaulted from 15 February 2018, leaving 16,999.85$ (sixteen thousand nine hundred ninety-nine dollars and eighty-five cents) outstanding, interest and principal alike unpaid since. [Para 11][Para 12] The institution sued for the balance, 1.6% monthly interest, a penalty of twice that rate under clause 4.1, 8,000,000 riel (eight million riel) for distress, and provisional enforcement. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether admitted, undisputed facts of the loan and the outstanding balance may be taken as proved without further evidence, under Article 123 K (2) of the Code of Civil Procedure, and the guarantor held liable with the borrowers? [Para 30][Para 47]
2. Whether a contractual penalty set at twice the monthly interest rate, which the debtors admitted signing but refused to pay as excessive, should be enforced, and on what basis are distress damages assessed? [Para 33][Para 36][Para 41]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the institution, through its authorised representative):** the debtors signed the loan and mortgage contracts of 05 November 2015, received the 25,000$, paid only in part and defaulted from 15 February 2018; the contract itself fixes the 1.6% rate and the double-rate penalty, and provisional enforcement is needed because the debtors may sell the mortgaged land and delay only increases the interest. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 25] It also claims 8,000,000 riel for wasted time travelling to the debtors and the effect on its cash flow. [Para 22]
**Defendants (Mr C1, Ms C2 and their guarantor daughter Ms C3):** they admit the contract, the borrowing and the 16,999.85$ balance, but refuse the double-rate penalty as excessive and refuse the 8,000,000 riel as beyond their means; they ask for time to sell land to pay, and oppose forced sale of the collateral, preferring to sell it themselves at a fair valuation. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 20][Para 26][Para 27]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** all three defendants must pay the institution 16,999.85$ principal, 1.6% monthly interest on that sum from 15 February 2018 until payment, a penalty equal to twice the 1.6% monthly rate on the same principal from the same date, and 2,000,000 riel (two million riel) in distress damages — the 8,000,000 riel claimed being reduced; point 1 of the order (the principal) is provisionally enforceable under Article 196 CPC, and costs fall on the defendants under Article 65 CPC. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
The court held that the debtors' admissions of the loan, the contract of 05 November 2015, the 16,999.85$ balance and the guarantor's undertaking made those facts undisputed, so they could be acted on without evidence under Article 123 K (2) CPC, and the guarantor was bound to pay in the debtor's stead. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 47] The 1.6% monthly rate was upheld as consistent with Article 584 K (2) of the Civil Code, Articles 4 and 5 of the 1996 Law on the Organisation and Conduct of the National Bank of Cambodia, and Prakas No. B7-09-213 of 09 September 2009, and because the debtors had accepted it in the contract. [Para 31][Para 32] The refusal to pay the double-rate penalty was rejected: the parties had agreed it in clause 4.1 and it was evidenced by the contract itself. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The distress claim succeeded in principle — the default genuinely affected the institution's interests and cash flow — but was excessive, so the court assessed it on the actual prejudice and awarded 2,000,000 riel. [Para 41][Para 48] Provisional enforcement was confined to the principal alone, as the priority debt the debtors admitted. [Para 44][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Facts admitted by the debtor and guarantor at the hearing — the loan, the contract, the outstanding balance and the guarantee — are undisputed facts on which the court may decide without further evidence under Article 123 K (2) CPC; a penalty clause fixed at twice the contractual monthly rate, agreed in the contract and not denied, will be enforced as written, while a claim for distress damages, though well founded in principle, is assessed on the actual prejudice and cut down where excessive. [Para 30][Para 36][Para 41][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that delay in repayment increases the interest burden and that the debtors' wish to sell the mortgaged land themselves at a fair valuation rather than suffer forced sale does not defeat provisional enforcement of the admitted principal. [Para 25][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The interest rate is printed as 1.6% throughout but as "(មួយ ក្បៀស ប្រាំ មួយ ភាគរយ)" — 1.51% — in the narrative of the loan at paragraph 10. [Para 10][Para 16] The debtors are numbered inconsistently across the same judgment as "០១/០២/០៣", "ច១/ច២/ច៣" and once "GM", and the loan date appears as 03 November 2015 in the summary but 05 November 2015 in the contract and reasoning. [Para 3][Para 10][Para 28][Para 31] The plaintiff's representative is named variously "ត ដ", "គង", "តដ ដ", "តង" and "តផ" in the operative paragraphs. [Para 2][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Amounts are printed in the continental style ("១៦.៩៩៩,៨៥"), and the judgment number itself is partly illegible ("..១៣… (ខ)"). [Para 1][Para 4]
