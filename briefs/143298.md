# Microfinance Institution (name redacted) v Ms C1, Mr C1-2, Ms C2 and Mr C2-2 (Civil Judgment No. 11.2 K, Oddar Meanchey Provincial Court, 25 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Kol Kompul, Vice-President) · first instance · Doc ID 143298

## 1. Facts
On 30 October 2015 Ms C1 and her husband Mr C1-2 borrowed 10,000$ (ten thousand US dollars) from the plaintiff at 1.7% (one point seven per cent) per month over 48 months, paying principal and interest every four months under a schedule of the same date, with a penalty of 3% per month on the outstanding principal if payment fell due after the final maturity of 30 October 2019; Ms C2 and her husband Mr C2-2 guaranteed the debt. [Para 11][Para 14] The borrowers paid instalments but stopped paying interest from 07 November 2017, leaving 4,946.19$ (four thousand nine hundred forty-six and nineteen cents) outstanding. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 27] The institution sued for that balance, 1.7% monthly interest, the 3% monthly penalty, 3,000$ (three thousand US dollars) for distress and lost time, and provisional enforcement. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 9][Para 10]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the borrowers' admissions of the loan contract, the outstanding 4,946.19$ and the failure to pay since 07 November 2017 dispense the court from requiring evidence, under Article 123 K (2) of the Code of Civil Procedure? [Para 28][Para 40]
2. Whether the guarantee extends to the guarantors' liability for principal, interest and penalty, including a guarantor who did not appear, and how the penalty and the distress claim are to be fixed? [Para 34][Para 37]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the institution, through Mr T, aged 34):** the borrowers signed the loan and security contracts of 30 October 2015, defaulted on interest from 07 November 2017, and the contract fixes both the 1.7% rate and the 3% penalty; the guarantors accepted joint liability and should pay if the borrowers cannot; provisional enforcement of the principal is needed to stop the loss deepening, and 3,000$ is claimed for the time and expense of pursuing the case under clause 10 of the contract. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 35]
**Defendants (borrowers Ms C1 and Mr C1-2; guarantors Ms C2 and Mr C2-2):** the borrowers admit the contract, the guarantee, the balance and the default, but reject the 3,000$ distress claim as excessive and beyond their means, deny that they lack the ability to pay, and ask for time to raise the money, opposing provisional enforcement for that reason; the guarantors accept the terms of the guarantee and agree to pay in the borrowers' stead. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 23][Para 25][Para 33][Para 36]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the borrowers must pay the institution 4,946.19$ principal, interest at 1.7% per month from 07 November 2017 until payment, and a penalty of 3% per month on the principal from 30 October 2019 (the final maturity date) until payment; the guarantors Ms C2 and Mr C2-2 are ordered to pay the same principal, interest and penalty in the event the borrowers cannot; the borrowers must also pay 2,000,000 riel (two million riel) in distress damages, the 3,000$ claimed being reduced; point 1 of the order is provisionally enforceable under Article 196 CPC, and costs fall on the defendants. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]
The court took the admitted facts as established without evidence under Article 123 K (2) CPC: the contract, the borrowing, the security contract and the balance were all conceded at the hearing. [Para 28][Para 40] The 1.7% rate was upheld under Article 584 K (2) of the Civil Code, Articles 4 and 5 of the 1996 National Bank of Cambodia law, and Prakas No. B7-09-213 of 09 September 2009, and because the debtors had accepted it in the contract. [Para 30] The 3% penalty was likewise enforced as an agreed term, running from the contractual final maturity. [Para 32][Para 44] On the guarantee, Ms C2 accepted the guarantee contract of 30 October 2015 and the guarantors' joint obligation; Mr C2-2 did not attend but was duly summoned, so his silence was treated as admission of the facts and the claim. [Para 34] The distress claim was well founded — the default really did affect the institution's interests and cash flow — but excessive, so it was assessed on the actual prejudice at 2,000,000 riel. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where borrowers and guarantors admit the loan contract, the security contract, the outstanding balance and the default, the court decides on those admitted facts without requiring evidence under Article 123 K (2) CPC, enforces the contractual monthly interest and the agreed penalty from the date the contract fixes for it, and holds the guarantors jointly liable to pay in the borrowers' stead, including a guarantor who, though duly summoned, fails to appear; a distress claim is allowed in principle but assessed on the actual prejudice. [Para 28][Para 34][Para 37][Para 40][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the borrowers' assertion of an ability to pay, and their request for time, does not defeat provisional enforcement of a principal they admit, since delay only worsens the institution's position. [Para 39][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The penalty is pleaded and awarded to run from 30 October 2019, a date after the judgment of 25 July 2019, because it is keyed to the contract's final maturity — the judgment nowhere explains how a future date is applied. [Para 7][Para 31][Para 44] The principal is printed variously as "៤,៩៤៦.១៩", "៤,៩ ៤៦.១៩" and "៤,៩៤៦.១៩." (with a stray full stop), and the words of amount at paragraph 24 read "បួន ពាន់ ប្រាំ បួន រយ" — "four thousand five four hundred" — instead of five hundred. [Para 5][Para 21][Para 24][Para 27] The default is said to run from 07 November 2017 although the loan was signed on 30 October 2015 and repayable every four months, with no explanation of the gap. [Para 11][Para 17] The last paragraph names only three defendants, not four. [Para 45][Para 51]
