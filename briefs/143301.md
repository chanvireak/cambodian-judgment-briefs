# Mr D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 18 Ch, Siem Reap Provincial Court, 10 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Muy Sopheak) · first instance · Doc ID 143301

## 1. Facts
On 04 December 2014 two borrowers (referred to only as "name 09" and "name 2") took 29,000,000 riel (twenty-nine million riel) from Mr D, and seven days later a further 5,000,000 riel (five million riel), 34,000,000 riel (thirty-four million riel) in all. [Para 10] Being also indebted to Ms C, they transferred land and a house to her in payment, and Ms C in turn undertook to settle the 34,000,000 riel with Mr D, executed a transfer of possession of land and house in his favour, paid him 8,000,000 riel (eight million riel), and signed a repayment contract of 01 January 2015 for the remaining 26,000,000 riel (twenty-six million riel). [Para 11][Para 12] She then refused to perform, and Mr D sued on 28 January 2016 for the balance, 16,000,000 riel (sixteen million riel) in damages, provisional enforcement and costs. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 13]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a third party who undertakes to pay another's debt, pays part of it and signs a repayment contract for the balance is bound to that balance under Articles 578 and 579 of the Civil Code, the original borrowers' debt having been transferred to her? [Para 31]
2. Whether damages for distress and lost time should be refused on account of the debtor's economic circumstances, and whether provisional enforcement should be ordered where an appeal could still alter the decision? [Para 36][Para 38]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, through his authorised lawyer):** he maintains the claim of 28 January 2016 and his statements in the preparatory proceedings of 07 April 2016 — 26,000,000 riel of principal, 16,000,000 riel for distress, lost time and other services, provisional enforcement of point 1, and costs and taxes on the defendant. [Para 14]
**Defendant (Ms C):** she admits owing the 26,000,000 riel and admits the plaintiff's account of the facts, but asks the court to reject claims 2, 3 and 4, pleading that she has no means to pay. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 34]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claims at points 2 and 3 of the 28 January 2016 plaint — the 16,000,000 riel damages and provisional enforcement — are dismissed; Ms C is ordered to pay Mr D 26,000,000 riel of principal, and to bear the court costs and taxes. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]
The court found the plaint regular in form and, on the statements and documentary evidence — the house-transfer contract of 15 December 2014, four repayment contracts of 01 January 2015, the joint purchase agreement, a tontine contract of 03 September 2014 and a loan repayment schedule of 13 December 2014 — held that Ms C's undertaking and part-payment bound her for the balance under Articles 578 and 579 of the Civil Code. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 24][Para 25][Para 27][Para 31] Since she admitted the debt, the principal was ordered as claimed. [Para 30][Para 32] The damages claim was refused: although she was at fault in not performing, the court weighed her economic situation and the size of the loss against her livelihood and found the claim excessive, dismissing it as "without basis for payment". [Para 35][Para 36] Provisional enforcement was refused because under Article 350 CPC such an order operates like a final judgment, so it must be considered with the greatest care, and an appeal could still vary the very point made enforceable, harming both sides. [Para 38][Para 39] Costs followed the event under Article 64 CPC. [Para 41]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A third party who, in consideration of a transfer of the debtor's property, undertakes to pay the debt, pays part of it and signs a repayment contract for the balance is personally bound for that balance under Articles 578 and 579 of the Civil Code, and the creditor may sue her directly; but a claim for distress and lost time may be refused where the court weighs the debtor's economic situation and livelihood against the loss, and provisional enforcement will be refused where an appeal could still alter the point ordered enforceable. [Para 31][Para 36][Para 38]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that an order of provisional enforcement takes effect like a final judgment under Article 350 CPC and therefore demands the most careful consideration — a statement of approach, not a finding on the debt. [Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
All party names are reduced to bare numbers or initials — the borrowers as "ឈ្មោះ ០៩" and "ឈ្មោះ ២", the further guarantors as "ឈ្មោះ ៣" to "ឈ្មោះ ៨", and the parties as "ដ" and "ច" — and the evidence list is so fragmented by the OCR that four separate repayment contracts of 01 January 2015 have their parties scattered across paragraphs. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 23] The court states at paragraph 39 that it dismisses the claim "at point 1", whereas point 1 is the admitted principal and the dispositive order dismisses only points 2 and 3; the passage evidently concerns provisional enforcement, which is point 3. [Para 39][Para 42] The amount in words is corrupted in two places ("សាម្សិបបួនលាន" for thirty-four million; "ម្ភៃ ប្រាំ មួយ លាន" for twenty-six million). [Para 12][Para 43] The interest evidence includes a tontine contract of 03 September 2014 that is never mentioned in the reasoning. [Para 24]
