# Mr D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 17 "ឈ", Siem Reap Provincial Court, 10 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Muy Sophak) · first instance · Doc ID 143302

## 1. Facts
Ms C borrowed from Mr D three times in August 2018: 4,000,000 riel (four million riel) on 02 August on an invoice only, with no contract; 500 USD (five hundred) on 25 August under a written contract; and a further 4,000,000 riel (four million riel) on 29 August under a written contract repayable at 200,000 riel (two hundred thousand riel) a day over 22 days. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] She made eight days of payments and then stopped. [Para 12] She later brought seven friends to borrow from Mr D — 500, 650, 930, 350, 500, 500 and 450 USD, a claimed 5,980 USD (five thousand nine hundred and eighty) in all — standing as their guarantor and collecting their repayments for Mr D. [Para 13][Para 14] Against all of that she had repaid 1,000,000 riel (one million riel) only. [Para 14]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a guarantor debtor who admits part only of a claimed USD debt is liable for the admitted sum where the creditor holds written loan contracts and invoices for the rest? [Para 41][Para 46]
2. Whether a debtor in default under a loan contract is liable for the creditor's lost time and distress, and whether the judgment should be declared provisionally enforceable? [Para 42][Para 43]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, lender):** every borrowing is documented and Ms C signed as guarantor; where she did not attend in person he telephoned her each time to confirm whether the money should be released to the borrower. [Para 22] He maintained the claim in full. [Para 27]
**Defendant (Ms C, borrower and guarantor):** she admits the borrowing but disputes three items — that she has repaid nearly all of the 650 USD owed by one friend, that she personally paid another friend's 350 USD to Mr D, and that of a third friend's 450 USD she owes only 200 USD — leaving 4,530 USD (four thousand five hundred and thirty), which she asks to pay at 100 USD (one hundred) a month. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is partly allowed: Ms C must pay 4,530 USD (four thousand five hundred and thirty) principal and 2,000,000 riel (two million riel) for lost time and distress, the balance of the claim being dismissed; provisional enforcement is refused, and costs and taxes fall on her as the losing party. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]
Under Articles 578 and 579 of the Civil Code the court weighed the invoices of 01 and 23 August 2018 and the loan contracts of 25 and 29 August 2018 against Ms C's admissions, and held her admission that she owes 4,530 USD credible — the higher figure of 5,980 USD being unsupported once her specific repayments were accounted for. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] On damages the court found her at fault for failing to perform, but attributed the default to a downturn in her economic circumstances and, under Articles 398 and 401 of the Civil Code, scaled the award to the extent of the loss, her means and her livelihood, reducing the 12,000,000 riel (twelve million riel) claimed to 2,000,000 riel. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Provisional enforcement was refused because under Article 350 of the Code of Civil Procedure a judgment declared provisionally enforceable takes effect as a final judgment, so an appeal that later varied it would prejudice both sides. [Para 55] Costs follow the event under Article 64. [Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A creditor suing on a series of loan contracts and invoices recovers only the sum the debtor admits and the documents corroborate; a claim above the admitted figure fails where the creditor cannot answer the debtor's specific evidence of repayment. [Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** that provisional enforcement is equivalent to a final judgment and must therefore be considered with the utmost care, since any variation on appeal would affect the interests of both parties. [Para 55]
