# Microfinance institution D v Ms C1, Mr C2, Ms C3 and Mr C4 (Civil Judgment No. 14 "ឆ", Takeo Provincial Court, 3 October 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Kong Phumint) · first instance · Doc ID 143303

## 1. Facts
On 18 January 2018 the four co-defendants borrowed 17,000 USD (seventeen thousand) from microfinance institution D, represented by Mr T D, at 1.4% a month over 72 months under a written contract, repayable monthly according to a schedule dated 01 August 2018, and secured by mortgages over land given by C1 and her husband C2 and by C3 and her husband C4. [Para 13][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] They paid for six months only — 2,587 USD (two thousand five hundred and eighty-seven), being 1,416 principal, 1,131 interest and 39 penalty — leaving 15,584 USD (fifteen thousand five hundred and eighty-four) of principal outstanding. [Para 14]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether borrowers who admit the debt and plead only inability to pay escape liability for contractual interest and penalty? [Para 37][Para 40]
2. Whether a penalty of 0.1% a day on overdue principal and 0.2% a day on overdue interest is enforceable as agreed, and whether the judgment should be declared provisionally enforceable? [Para 39][Para 42]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (institution D, lender):** the co-defendants signed the contract and the mortgages, the arrears are documented, and the offer of 1,000,000 riel a month is far below what the debt requires and is refused. [Para 22][Para 26]
**Defendants (the four co-borrowers and mortgagors):** they admit the claim entirely — they did borrow and did repay part — but ask to pay only 1,000,000 riel (one million riel) a month because they are heavily indebted to others. [Para 27]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed as to principal, interest and a reduced penalty, and partly allowed as to damages: the four co-defendants must pay jointly 15,584 USD principal, interest at 1.4% a month from 17 August 2018 until paid, a penalty of 0.07% a day on overdue principal and 0.07% a day on overdue interest from 17 August 2018 until paid, and 3,000,000 riel (three million riel) for lost time and expenses; provisional enforcement is granted on the principal only, and costs fall on the defendants. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
The court first held the claim admissible under Articles 74 and 75 of the Code of Civil Procedure, then found the borrowing proved by the loan contract, the two mortgage contracts of 18 January 2018 and the defendants' own admission of the 15,584 USD principal. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] The 1.4% monthly rate was upheld under Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code and clause 1 of Prakas No. 128 កយប្រក/១១ of 21 December 2011. [Para 38] The penalty clause fared differently: 0.1% a day on principal and 0.2% a day on interest exceeded what clause 2 of the same Prakas allows, so the court substituted the statutory rate on each head. [Para 39][Para 40] The 8,000,000 riel (eight million riel) claimed for lost time and expenses was found excessive and unsupported by evidence, and was reduced to 3,000,000 riel on the actual circumstances. [Para 41][Para 48] Provisional enforcement was granted on the principal because the defendants had admittedly received the money, and refused as to the rest. [Para 42][Para 50] Cost follows the event under Article 64. [Para 43][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A liquidated penalty in a loan contract that exceeds the ceiling set by the Prakas on interest rates is not struck down; the court reduces it to the statutory maximum and enforces it at that rate, leaving the contractual interest rate untouched where it is within the cap. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** that provisional enforcement may be ordered on the principal alone while being withheld from interest, penalty and damages, because the receipt of the principal is admitted and needs no further proof. [Para 42]
