# Microfinance institution D v Ms C1 and others (Civil Judgment No. 344 K, Tboung Khmum Provincial Court, 8 October 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Pon Vanna) · first instance · Doc ID 143311

## 1. Facts
Microfinance institution D lent the debtor family twice: USD 40,000 (forty thousand) under a contract of 19 July 2017 for 48 months at 1.15% interest and 2% penalty per month, disbursed 26 July 2017 and expressed to mature on 26 July 2024, and USD 1,500 (one thousand five hundred) under a contract of 20 March 2018 for 24 months at 1.50% per month, disbursed 30 March 2018 and maturing 30 March 2020; Ms C3 and her husband stood as guarantors on both. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24][Para 25] By the filing date of 07 August 2019 USD 9,044 of principal had been repaid on the first loan and USD 945 on the second, leaving USD 30,956 (thirty thousand nine hundred and fifty-six) and USD 555 (five hundred and fifty-five), with USD 1,244.97 of late interest. [Para 23][Para 31][Para 27] The claim was for those sums plus interest, penalty, USD 8,000 lost-time damages, costs and provisional enforcement. [Para 10][Para 17][Para 19]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the claims for contractual interest and penalty (ប្រាក់ពិន័យ) on each loan are correct in rate and period? [Para 35]
2. Whether the defendants must pay USD 8,000 in legal service, lost time and distress damages? [Para 35]
3. Whether the judgment should be declared provisionally enforceable? [Para 35]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (microfinance institution D, creditor):** clauses 1.3 and 3.1 of both contracts fix interest and penalty, the defendants are in default, and the penalty will be pressed if the term runs out unpaid; the institution needs cash flow and cannot leave debt stagnant, and its figures rest on documents and do not vary. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40]
**Defendants (Ms C1, Ms C2 and Ms C3 with their husbands; borrowers and guarantor):** they admit both contracts, the rates, the 48- and 24-month terms and the arrears, but plead inability to pay and ask that no provisional enforcement issue, because it would lead to seizure and quick sale of mortgaged land to the detriment of their families. [Para 29][Para 32][Para 38][Para 41]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants are ordered jointly to pay, on loan 1, USD 30,956 (thirty thousand nine hundred and fifty-six) principal, USD 1,244.97 late interest, interest at 1.15% per month from 08 August 2019 to 26 July 2024 and penalty at 2% per month from 27 July 2024 until paid; and on loan 2, USD 555 (five hundred and fifty-five) principal, interest at 1.5% per month from 08 August 2019 to 30 March 2020 and penalty at 2% per month from 31 March 2020 until paid; the rest of the claim is dismissed, costs lie with the defendants, and points 1(a) and 1(b) are provisionally enforceable. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]
Under Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 128 K.Y. of 21 December 2011 the court treated 18% a year as the interest ceiling and 27% a year as the penalty ceiling, and found the contractual rates within them. [Para 43] The periods claimed were nonetheless wrong: computed as pleaded, the creditor would take interest and penalty over the same months, one on top of the other. [Para 44] Interest therefore runs from the date of default to the contractual maturity date, and the penalty only from the day after maturity until payment, loan by loan; the court said this expressly for each contract. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] The USD 8,000 was held to be a claim for moral damages under Article 400 of the Civil Code, which protects personality interests, not a money debt; the harm was economic and already repaired by interest and penalty. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] Provisional enforcement followed for the principal, the documents being sufficient and the debt admitted. [Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where several loan contracts each provide interest for default and a penalty for late payment, the court must compute each contract separately and in sequence — interest to that contract's maturity date, penalty only thereafter — so that a creditor within the statutory rate caps still cannot recover twice for the same period of delay. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 48][Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** Article 400 of the Civil Code is confined to infringements of personality interests — reputation, life and body — and does not reach economic loss already compensated by interest and penalty. [Para 52][Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The prayer claims USD 1,688.56 of late interest to 07 August 2019, while the statement of facts and the operative order both give USD 1,244.97 for the same period; both figures are reported. [Para 13][Para 23][Para 62] The reasoning states the rate on loan 1 as 1.50% per month in two places, while the contract, the prayer and the order give 1.15%. [Para 43][Para 47][Para 60] Loan 1 is dated 19 July 2017 with a 48-month term but a maturity date of 26 July 2024 (84 months). [Para 21] Loan 2 is dated 20 March 2018 in the facts, 20 March 2017 in the reasoning, and 03 March 2018 in the operative part. [Para 24][Para 49][Para 57] The filing year is printed "២១០៩" (2109) for 2019, and the USD 8,000 is glossed "(មួយ ម៉ឺន)" (ten thousand) in one passage. [Para 23][Para 54] The watermark "សាលក្រម នេះ ជារឿងក្តី ជាក់ស្តែង..." is spliced into paras 10, 23, 37, 47, 56 and 68. Parties survive only as C1, C2, C3 with spouses and the lender as "ដ" (D); kept as redactions. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]
