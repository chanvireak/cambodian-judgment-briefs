# Microfinance institution D v Mr C1 and five others (Civil Judgment No. 39 "N", Kratie Provincial Court, 28 June 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Nhil Bunly) · first instance · Doc ID 143315

## 1. Facts
Mr C1 and his wife Ms C2, borrowers and mortgagors, took two loans from microfinance institution D: USD 40,000 (forty thousand) on 02 June 2014 over 48 months at 1.5% a month with a 3% monthly penalty, final maturity 08 June 2018; and USD 50,000 (fifty thousand) on 16 June 2015 over 60 months at 1.4% a month with the same 3% penalty, final maturity 10 July 2020. [Para 18] Ms C4 with her husband Mr C3 guaranteed the first loan and Ms C6 with her husband Mr C5 the second. [Para 7][Para 8] By the filing date USD 92,672.38 had been paid in all, of which USD 52,997.71 was applied to loan 1, leaving USD 6,120.71 of principal on that loan and USD 30,000 on loan 2. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The institution also claimed USD 8,000 for lost time, costs and provisional enforcement. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the defendants' inability to repay the debt is a ground releasing them from their obligation? [Para 24]
2. Whether the contractual interest of 1.5% and 1.4% a month and the 3% monthly penalty are recoverable as agreed? [Para 53][Para 56]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (microfinance institution D, creditor):** it maintains its claim against all six defendants as pleaded, having tried repeatedly to settle and allowed time for payment without result. [Para 24][Para 25]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2 borrowers; Mr C3, Ms C4, Mr C5 and Ms C6 guarantors):** Mr C1 admitted every head of the claim and asked only for time, saying he farms three hectares of vegetables and will harvest next month and plant Korean beans in March 2019, and asking that the USD 8,000 be waived; the other five were duly summoned and did not appear. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 28][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Mr C1, Ms C2 and the guarantors Mr C3 and Ms C4 are ordered jointly and severally to pay on loan 1 USD 6,120.71 principal, interest at 1.5% a month and a penalty of 3% a month, both from 08 June 2018 until payment; Mr C1, Ms C2 and the guarantors Mr C5 and Ms C6 are ordered jointly and severally to pay on loan 2 USD 30,000 principal, interest at 1.4% a month and a penalty of 3% a month, both from 11 June 2018 until payment; all six are ordered jointly to pay USD 2,000 (two thousand) for legal service, lost time and procedural costs; the remainder of the claim is dismissed; points (a) and (b) are provisionally enforceable and costs lie with the defendants. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]
The court found both loan contracts, the guarantee contracts and the securities genuine, with interest and penalty written clearly, satisfying Articles 579 and 583 of the Civil Code and Book 6, Chapter 7 on guarantee. [Para 46][Para 47] Acknowledgment by the debtor disposed of the principal and interest, so no further proof was needed; the court could proceed against the absent guarantors, who had been properly summoned. [Para 52][Para 53] Default on a debt does not discharge the debtor, whatever the cause of the inability to pay. [Para 24][Para 53] The 1.5% and 1.4% monthly rates were tested against Article 585 of the Civil Code, Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 128, which caps interest at 18% a year, and were upheld. [Para 54][Para 55] The 3% monthly penalty (36% a year) was likewise upheld as a valid pre-agreed sum, the court holding that no instrument yet caps the rate of such compensation in the banking and finance sector, so the parties' agreement governs. [Para 56][Para 57] The USD 8,000 was a real but unquantified loss, reduced to USD 2,000 on equitable grounds. [Para 58][Para 64] Provisional enforcement was granted on principal, interest and penalty because the debt was admitted and no security need be lodged under Article 196. [Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A debtor's inability to pay, however caused, does not extinguish the debt or release the guarantors, who remain liable jointly and severally on their own contracts; and where the agreed penalty is 3% a month, it is enforced as written because no instrument yet caps the rate of pre-agreed compensation in the banking and finance sector, the 18% ceiling applying only to interest. [Para 47][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** an unquantified procedural loss may be reduced on equitable grounds rather than refused outright. [Para 58][Para 64]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The payments credited to loan 1 are stated as USD 52,997.71, but its components — USD 33,979.29 principal, USD 18,621.45 interest and USD 496.97 penalty — add to USD 53,097.71. [Para 18] The USD 8,000 claim is glossed in words as "ពីរ ពាន់ ប្រាំរយ" (two thousand five hundred) in one passage while the sum ordered is USD 2,000. [Para 58][Para 64] On loan 2 the penalty is prayed only "in case of exceeding the final due date of 10 July 2020" yet ordered to run from 11 June 2018, almost two years before maturity. [Para 12][Para 63] The guarantee contract for loan 2 is dated 16 June 2014 in the evidence recital though the loan is of 16 June 2015. [Para 39][Para 47] The operative paragraph for loan 2 splices "ច៥" into the sentence naming the creditor. [Para 63] The watermark "សាលក្រម នេះ ជារឿងក្តី ជាក់ស្តែង..." is spliced into paras 9, 18, 26, 46, 55, 62 and 69, and paras 51–52 are scrambled by column break. Parties survive only as T D, C1–C6 and the institution as "ដ" (D); kept as redactions. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 7]
