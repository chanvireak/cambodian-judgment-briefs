# Ms D v Mr 01 and Madam 02 (Civil Judgment No. 1624 "J", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 25 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Sao Sophea) · first instance · Doc ID 143318

## 1. Facts
The defendants, a married couple (Mr 01, 47, and Madam 02, 36), borrowed from the plaintiff Ms D in three tranches: 2,000$ on 15 January 2015, 11,000$ on 26 September 2018 and 11,000$ on 26 October 2018, a total of 24,000$ (twenty-four thousand US dollars), agreeing to 5% interest per month and pledging their house and land title as security. [Para 10][Para 11] They repaid 3,900$ of principal, leaving 20,100$ (twenty thousand one hundred US dollars) outstanding, and paid no interest from 3 March 2019. [Para 12] In early February 2019 they asked for the title deed back to show a buyer and then avoided the plaintiff. [Para 13] The plaintiff sued on 19 August 2019 for principal, 2% monthly interest, a 10% penalty, 5,000$ in lawyer's fees and costs. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether three written loan contracts, uncontradicted by any document from the defendants, prove the debt and its amount? [Para 28][Para 30]
2. Whether a contractual interest rate of 2% per month may be enforced, or must be reduced to the statutory ceiling? [Para 32]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, lender):** she maintained her 19 August 2019 complaint and the three contracts, stated that 24,000$ was lent and only 3,900$ repaid, leaving 20,100$ plus interest unpaid, and asked for provisional enforcement of the principal. [Para 14][Para 15]
**Defendants (Mr 01 and Madam 02, borrowers):** Mr 01 admitted borrowing 2,000$ but said his wife repaid it in full under the contract of 15 January 2015; he denied liability altogether on the contracts of 30 August 2018 and 26 October 2018 (22,000$), saying he knew nothing of them, and rejected all six heads of claim. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed in part. The defendants must pay 20,100$ principal plus 1.5% monthly interest from the date of default; 5,000,000 riel (five million riel) for moral damages and other expenses; all further sums claimed are refused; the 10% penalty and provisional enforcement are refused; costs lie on the defendants under Article 64 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]
The court first held the complaint procedurally regular and admissible. [Para 23] On the evidence it found the claim proved by the three loan contracts of 15 January 2015, 30 August 2018 and 26 October 2018, the defendants having filed no document at all and called no witness. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 28] Mr 01's denial of the two later contracts was treated as a bare denial pleaded to escape liability, not as evidence, and his admission of the 2,000$ loan with repayment asserted through his wife did not discharge the balance. [Para 29] Under Articles 578 and 591 of the Civil Code the borrower is bound to repay principal and interest to the lender, so the outstanding 20,100$ was ordered against both spouses jointly. [Para 30][Para 31]
On interest, the court held the claimed 2% per month exceeded the lawful ceiling: under Articles 584 and 591 of the Civil Code and Ministry of Justice letter No. 128 KYK.PRK/11 of 11 December 2011, interest may not exceed 1.5% (one point five per cent) per month on the principal. [Para 32] The 10% annual penalty was left unconsidered and refused, as was provisional enforcement. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 40][Para 41] The claim for 5,000$ in fees and moral damages was allowed but capped at 5,000,000 riel, anything above being dismissed. [Para 35][Para 39]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A lender who produces written loan contracts that the borrower neither rebuts with any document nor displaces by evidence is entitled to judgment for the unpaid principal, the borrower's bare denial being no defence; but contractual interest above the 1.5% monthly ceiling set by Ministry of Justice letter No. 128 KYK.PRK/11 is cut down to that ceiling, not enforced as agreed. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 32]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a penalty clause and a request for provisional enforcement are discretionary matters the court may decline to consider. [Para 33][Para 34]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The second loan is dated 26 September 2018 in the narrative but the contract examined and relied on is dated 30 August 2018. [Para 10][Para 21] The interest order runs "from the date of default until 26 October 2018", which reverses the accrual period described in the reasoning as running from 26 October 2018. [Para 32][Para 38] The award is pleaded as 5,000$ but pronounced as 5,000,000 riel. [Para 35][Para 39] The plaintiff is recorded as male (ភេទ ប្រុស) while the pleading is voiced by a female attorney for "my client"; party names survive only as initials and digits (Ms D, Mr 01, Madam 02) and addresses are stripped — reported as OCR losses, not expanded.
