# Bank D v Mr C1 and Mr C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1544 R, Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 5 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Li Sokha) · first instance · Doc ID 143322

## 1. Facts
Under a loan agreement of 24 October 2018 the plaintiff bank lent the two defendants two facilities: USD 150,000 (one hundred fifty thousand US dollars) at 9% a year repayable in 180 monthly instalments of 1,533$, and 200,000,000 riel (two hundred million riel) at 9% a year repayable in 120 monthly instalments of 2,547,100 riel, secured by a mortgage over a three-storey house on land plot 1020, Khan Pur Senchey, under title certificate of 27 July 2017. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 18][Para 37] The agreement provided that three consecutive missed instalments would raise the rate from 9% to 24% a year. [Para 18][Para 43] The defendants stopped paying on 8 April 2019 (first facility) and 8 May 2019 (second), leaving USD 149,446.50 (one hundred forty-nine thousand four hundred forty-six and fifty cents) and 196,383,257 riel (one hundred ninety-six million three hundred eighty-three thousand two hundred fifty-seven riel) outstanding despite reminders of 24 December 2018, 18 March 2019 and 31 May 2019. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 23][Para 32][Para 41]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a contractual escalation of interest from 9% to 24% a year on default may be enforced, or must be reduced to the statutory ceiling? [Para 46]
2. Whether a 24% annual late-payment rate may stand, and whether a 15,000$ claim for lawyer's fees and travel may be awarded in full? [Para 51][Para 53]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Bank D, lender):** it relied on the loan and mortgage contracts of 24 October 2018, the reminder and demand letters of 24 December 2018, 18 March 2019, 31 May 2019 and 19 June 2019, the title certificate and its final debt schedule, and asked for termination of the contract, both outstanding principals, 24% annual interest, 24% annual late interest and 15,000$ in fees and expenses. [Para 18][Para 26][Para 28][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Mr C2, borrowers):** they entered no defence — they were served with the summons through the court's process server and did not appear at any stage, without giving any reason or seeking an adjournment. [Para 27][Para 39]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the loan agreement of 24 October 2018 is terminated. The defendants must pay USD 149,446.50 and 196,383,257 riel, with 18% annual interest on those sums from 8 May 2019 to 1 July 2019 and 24% annual late interest on them from 1 July 2019 until payment; 6,000,000 riel (six million riel) for lawyer's fees, travel and other expenses, the excess being dismissed; costs lie on the defendants; provisional enforcement is refused. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]
The court held the non-appearing defendants to have admitted the plaintiff's factual allegations under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure and, their default being unexplained, entered judgment against them in absentia. [Para 39][Para 40] On the documents it accepted that both facilities were created and that the stated balances remained due after part-payment. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 47][Para 48]
On the rate, the contract fixed 9% but escalated to 24% after three consecutive missed instalments. [Para 42][Para 43] Article 585 of the Civil Code allows the parties to fix interest but not above the statutory rate, and clause 1 of Ministry of Justice Prakas No. 128 KYK.PRK/11 caps it at 18% a year, so the agreed 24% was unlawful and was reduced to 18%, running from 8 May 2019 to 1 July 2019. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] The separate 24% late-payment rate for default, by contrast, was upheld as lawful under Article 403 of the Civil Code and Article 17(k), paragraph 6 of the Law on the Implementation of the Civil Code, and runs from 1 July 2019. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 58] The 15,000$ fee claim was found justified in principle but excessive, and was cut to 6,000,000 riel. [Para 53][Para 59][Para 60] Provisional enforcement was refused for want of necessity. [Para 55][Para 62]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contractual clause raising ordinary interest on default above the 18% annual ceiling of Prakas No. 128 is unenforceable and is reduced to 18%, but a distinct late-payment (penalty) rate agreed for default may be enforced at its agreed level under Article 403 of the Civil Code; and a claim for lawyer's fees and travel is assessed for reasonableness rather than awarded as pleaded. [Para 46][Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that provisional enforcement is a discretionary remedy to be refused where the claim shows no necessity for it. [Para 55]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Both facilities are said to default from 8 April 2019 in the narrative, but the award runs interest from 8 May 2019. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 42][Para 57] The interest end-date is printed as 1 July 2018 in the reasoning and 1 July 2019 in the order. [Para 49][Para 57] The riel principal appears as 196,383,257 in the prayer and award but 186,383,257 at one point in the reasoning, and the words beside USD 150,000 in the operative findings read "១ USD ៥០,០០០". [Para 36][Para 37][Para 57] The loan agreement is dated 24 October 2018 in the recitals but 24 October 2019 in the summary of undisputed facts. [Para 12][Para 14] Party names survive only as initials (C1, C2); the bank is given as "Bank D".
