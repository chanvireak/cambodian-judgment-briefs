# Microfinance Institution D v Mr C1 and Madam C2 (Civil Judgment No. 1599 "Kh", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 18 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Ros Sami Di) · first instance · Doc ID 143323

## 1. Facts
On 25 March 2016 Mr C1 and his wife Madam C2 borrowed 6,000$ (six thousand US dollars) from the plaintiff microfinance institution at 1.8% interest per month, repayable in principal and interest over 48 months. [Para 6][Para 8] They paid instalments for only 15 months and then stopped, despite repeated reminders and demands. [Para 7][Para 9] The institution sued on 17 May 2018 to terminate the contract and recover 4,125$ (four thousand one hundred twenty-five US dollars) principal, 18% annual interest from July 2017, a penalty of 2,143.83$ (two thousand one hundred forty-three US dollars and fifty-three cents), 2,000$ in damages, provisional enforcement and the court tax. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the outstanding principal of 4,125$ is owed where the defendants admit the loan but the institution produces no contract matching the one pleaded? [Para 14][Para 16]
2. Whether the claims to terminate the contract and to recover a 2,143.83$ penalty can succeed without any supporting evidence? [Para 18][Para 19]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Microfinance Institution D, lender):** through its authorised representative it pleaded the loan agreement, the 1.8% monthly rate, the 48-month schedule, 15 months of payments and the resulting balance of 4,125$, and claimed termination, interest, penalty, damages and provisional enforcement. [Para 8][Para 9]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Madam C2, borrowers):** Madam C2 admitted signing the 6,000$ loan at 1.8% per month and admitted defaulting, but asked to repay the balance at 100$ a month, asked for the interest to be reduced, and refused the 2,143.83$ penalty and the 2,000$ damages for want of any means to pay. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claims to terminate the contract and to recover the 2,143.83$ penalty are dismissed. The defendants must pay 4,125$ principal, 18% annual interest on it, and 1,000,000 riel (one million riel) for loss of time and other expenses, the excess refused; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]
The court found that the institution had filed no evidence proving its claim — the only contract in the file is dated 17 November 2016, not the 25 March 2016 agreement pleaded — but that both defendants admitted borrowing 6,000$ and admitted the balance of 4,125$ as claimed, so that head was accepted on the admission. [Para 14][Para 16][Para 17] The same reasoning carried the 18% interest: unproven by documents, but admitted by the defendants. [Para 17][Para 29]
Termination was refused because no evidence supported it, and the penalty likewise. [Para 18][Para 19] The court held the defendants' non-performance to be deliberate and so a fault engaging liability in damages under Articles 398(1) and 400 of the Civil Code, but awarded only what was reasonable, 1,000,000 riel. [Para 24][Para 30] Madam C2's absence was treated as admission of the plaintiff's factual allegations, and judgment was entered against her in absentia under Articles 201(2) and 202(k) of the Code of Civil Procedure, her husband having appeared and raised no objection. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] Provisional enforcement was granted for the principal to stop further loss. [Para 23][Para 31]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for repayment of a loan balance, and for contractual interest, may be upheld on the defendants' admission even where the claimant has failed to produce the contract pleaded; but claims to terminate the contract and to recover a penalty, being distinct remedies, require evidence and fail where none is produced. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]

**Obiter dicta:** the characterisation of non-payment of a debt as deliberate fault engaging liability under Articles 398(1) and 400 of the Civil Code, and the treatment of an absent spouse as admitting the claim where the other spouse appears without objection. [Para 21][Para 24]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The loan is dated 25 March 2016 in the prayer and throughout the reasoning, 23 March 2016 in the summary of facts, and 17 November 2016 on the contract actually filed. [Para 6][Para 14][Para 16] The principal is printed as "៦០,០០ $" — the figure reads sixty dollars while the words read six thousand. [Para 6] The contractual rate is 1.8% per month but the judgment awards 18% per year without explaining the conversion. [Para 6][Para 29] The interest start date of July 2017 is pleaded but no end date or computation is given. [Para 3] Party names appear only as C1 and C2, and several lines are truncated mid-sentence by OCR ("បន្ទុក ។"). [Para 25]
