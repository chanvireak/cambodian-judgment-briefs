# Bank v Mr C1 and Madam C2 (Civil Judgment No. 24 "K", Siem Reap Provincial Court, 27 June 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Sin Sovannarath) · first instance · Doc ID 143326

## 1. Facts
The plaintiff bank lent the defendants, husband and wife, successively under loan agreements of 10 February 2016, 5 April 2017 and 19 April 2018, with an overdraft of USD 70,000 (seventy thousand US dollars) later converted into a term loan, secured by a mortgage over land in Chreav commune, Siem Reap, under a chain of title documents from 22 May 2009 to 16 November 2018. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 18][Para 48] The defendants repaid only USD 39,000 of principal on the earlier facilities and USD 300 on the converted facility, then defaulted from 24 December 2018 and 25 February 2019. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16] After the claim was filed on 25 March 2019 they paid USD 12,776.47, of which USD 5,457.11 went to principal and USD 4,257.20 to interest. [Para 17][Para 19] The bank sued for USD 150,700 principal, 11.75% annual interest, a 25% annual penalty, USD 16,000 in fees and provisional enforcement. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the borrowers' admitted default entitles the bank to the outstanding principal and contractual interest under the loan agreements? [Para 42][Para 56][Para 57]
2. Whether the 25% annual penalty, the USD 16,000 claim for legal and procedural costs, and provisional enforcement should be granted? [Para 58][Para 61][Para 63]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (the bank):** it relied on the three loan agreements, the security documents and its ledger, and pleaded that the defendants defaulted in breach of the repayment schedule, revising its claim after post-filing payments to USD 150,700 principal with 11.75% interest on USD 81,000 from 4 June 2019 and on USD 69,700 from 26 May 2019, plus 25% penalties and USD 16,000 in costs. [Para 22][Para 29][Para 36]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Madam C2, borrowers and mortgagors):** Madam C2 admitted owing the full USD 150,700 principal, but asked to repay the interest according to the bank's ordinary repayment schedule, and asked the court to excuse the 25% penalty, the USD 16,000 in costs and provisional enforcement, pleading that they had resumed payment and requesting negotiation and indulgence. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants must pay USD 150,700 (one hundred fifty thousand seven hundred US dollars) principal; 11.75% annual interest on USD 81,000 from 4 June 2019 and on USD 69,700 from 26 May 2019; a 25% annual penalty on the same two sums, running from 24 December 2018 and 25 February 2019 respectively; and 2,000,000 riel (two million riel) for legal service fees, lost time and procedural expenses; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal; costs lie on the defendants. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]
The court found the breach established by the three agreements, the security contracts and the schedule, and by the defendants' own admission of the balance. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] Article 384 of the Civil Code requires the debtor to perform according to the contract and good faith; the debtors had not, and their conduct in admitting the breach and resuming payment per the bank's schedule was noted but did not excuse liability. [Para 54][Para 55]
Interest at 11.75% was upheld as within the agreed rate permitted by the Civil Code, on Articles 583, 586 and 591. [Para 52][Para 57] The 25% penalty was upheld because annex (k) of each agreement provided that on default the bank could claim it at that rate. [Para 58][Para 59] The USD 16,000 claim for legal costs, lost time and expenses was held justified in principle — the suit was necessitated by the defendants' breach — but its amount was assessed "according to the actual facts" and fixed at 2,000,000 riel. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 71] Provisional enforcement was granted on the principal under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure, the claim being one to rights over property. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A debtor who admits the outstanding principal and whose default is documented by the loan agreements is liable for that principal, for the agreed contractual interest within lawful limits, and for a penalty clause validly stipulated in an annex to the agreement; but a lump-sum claim for legal fees and lost time, though justified in principle, is reduced to the amount the court assesses as actually incurred. [Para 50][Para 57][Para 58][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a debtor's post-breach efforts at settlement and resumption of instalment payments are relevant to the court's assessment but do not cure the breach. [Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The bank pleads total additional lending of USD 120,000 with USD 39,000 repaid, yet claims USD 150,700 principal, and no reconciliation appears. [Para 13][Para 66] The USD 150,700 figure is printed as "USD១៥០.៧០០,០០" — dollars and cents punctuated in the Khmer style — and the words beside it read "one hundred fifty thousand seven hundred". [Para 6][Para 66] The interest and penalty bases are split USD 81,000 / USD 69,700 without explaining how the split was derived. [Para 30][Para 31] Some security documents are dated 2011 and 2012, before the 2016 loan. [Para 18] Party names survive only as C1 and C2; the bank's name is reduced to "ដ" and a bare initial.
