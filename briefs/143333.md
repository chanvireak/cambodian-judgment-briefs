# Microfinance Institution D v Ms C1, Mr C2 and Ms C3 (Civil Judgment No. 21 K, Takeo Provincial Court, 17 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Sung Vuthi) · first instance · Doc ID 143333

## 1. Facts
By two separate contracts both dated 10 November 2016 the institution lent 2,000,000 (two million) riel to Ms C1 and her husband Mr C2, and a further 2,000,000 riel to Ms C3 (aged 67), each repayable with 2.9% monthly interest over twelve months and each secured by a mortgage over immovable property. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 30][Para 33]
Ms C1 and Mr C2 paid eight months of interest — 534,600 riel — and stopped on 17 August 2017; Ms C3 paid three months — 225,000 riel — and stopped on 17 March 2017. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 32] The institution sued for the 4,000,000 riel principal, 2.9% monthly interest, daily penalties of 0.1% on principal and 0.2% on interest, and a total of 4,000,000 riel in damages. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a contractual rate of 2.9% per month, and daily penalties of 0.1% and 0.2%, are enforceable under the Civil Code where the borrowers admit the loans? [Para 33][Para 34][Para 36][Para 40]
2. Whether the two debtor units — a married couple and a separate elderly woman under their own contracts — are each liable on their own debt, and in what amount of damages? [Para 31][Para 39][Para 40][Para 43]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr T D, aged 34, representative of the institution):** two written contracts of 10 November 2016, each thumbprinted by the debtors before witnesses, establish the loans; the debtors defaulted after paying only part of the interest, causing the institution loss of profit, so principal, contractual interest, penalties and 4,000,000 riel in damages are claimed. [Para 18][Para 31][Para 33]
**Defendants (Ms C1 and Mr C2; Ms C3):** all three admit signing for 2,000,000 riel each, the twelve-month term, the 2.9% monthly interest, and that they stopped paying interest; they ask that the penalties and other damages be deleted and that they be allowed to repay principal and interest within 3,000,000 riel because they cannot afford more. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 27][Para 29]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed in part. Ms C1 and Mr C2 must jointly pay 2,000,000 riel principal, interest at 18 (eighteen) per cent a year on that principal from 17 August 2017 until payment, and 1,000,000 (one million) riel in damages; Ms C3 must pay 2,000,000 riel principal, interest at 18% a year from 17 March 2017, and 1,000,000 riel in damages; all further claims are dismissed, costs lie on the defendants, and the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
The court held the complaint regular and admissible, then found the two contracts clear, freely agreed, thumbprinted and witnessed, so that all three defendants had in fact borrowed from the institution — a conclusion confirmed by their own statements. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] It recorded the interest actually paid by each debtor, found that the debtors had failed to perform and had thereby caused the creditor loss of profit, and cited Articles 398 and 400 of the Civil Code, under which a creditor may claim damages for lost benefit and compensation for mental distress. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 36][Para 37] The operative order then fixed interest at 18% a year in place of the 2.9% monthly rate, awarded 1,000,000 riel per debtor unit, and declared the principal provisionally enforceable. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a creditor claims interest at a monthly rate far above the statutory ceiling, the court will enter judgment for the principal and substitute interest at 18% per annum running from the date of default, and will award assessed damages rather than the penalties and damages as pleaded; each separate loan contract is enforced against its own debtor unit, spouses being jointly liable on theirs. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** that a contract freely agreed, thumbprinted and witnessed suffices to prove the loan even where the debtors later plead inability to pay, and that loss of profit and mental distress are recoverable under Articles 398 and 400 of the Civil Code. [Para 31][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Interest was cut from 2.9% per month (about 34.8% a year) to 18% a year, and both daily penalties (0.1% on principal, 0.2% on interest) were dropped, with no provision or reason stated anywhere in the reasoning. [Para 6][Para 8][Para 40][Para 43] The interest totals contradict the months said to have been paid: eight months at 2.9% of 2,000,000 riel is 464,000 riel, not the 534,600 riel recorded, and three months is 174,000 riel, not 225,000 riel. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 32] Ms C3 is 67 at [Para 5] and 65 at [Para 43]. [Para 5][Para 43] Her redacted initial is replaced once by what appears to be a real name at [Para 39]; it is not reproduced here. [Para 39] The default year is printed as "207" at [Para 21], the complaint paragraph is scrambled at [Para 30], and the page-header disclaimer bleeds into sentences at [Para 8], [Para 17], [Para 29] and [Para 39]. [Para 8][Para 17][Para 21][Para 30][Para 39] The court cites Articles 7 and 8 of the Civil Code for admissibility where Article 75 of the Code of Civil Procedure would be expected. [Para 30]
