# Ms D v Mr C1, Ms C1-2 and others (Civil Judgment No. 75/03 "K", Preah Sihanouk Provincial Court, 11 September 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Sok Heng) · first instance · Doc ID 143335

## 1. Facts
Ms D says she bought a plot of 64 m by 210 m at Preah Sihanouk in 1991 from a soldier, Mr 9, and held it for 26 years on the strength of a village-chief application for possession (ពាក្យ ស្នើសុំ កាន់កាប់ ដីធ្លី); in 2006 the defendants Mr C1 and his wife Ms C1-2, with Mr C2 and others, put up huts and a fence on it, and in 2016 they sold it to Mr C7 and obtained a title certificate of 30 June 2016 over 14,065 (fourteen thousand and sixty-five) m². [Para 11][Para 13][Para 8][Para 17]
A 2013 criminal judgment (No. 38 "K") had already jailed Mr C1 and Mr C2 for six months for violence against a lawful possessor of immovable property. [Para 17][Para 19] Ms D sued for recognition of ownership and registration of a transfer to her name, with costs. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether an unregistered 1991 sale of immovable property, evidenced by a later written acknowledgment and an application for possession, transfers ownership against a defendant holding a title certificate issued through the land authorities? [Para 69][Para 72][Para 73]
2. Whether a certificate obtained after the provincial governor's decision of 11 December 2015, and the defendants' later registered transfer to Mr C7, are valid under Article 65 of the Land Law in the absence of proof of fraud? [Para 75][Para 78]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, represented by attorney (name redacted)):** she bought the land in 1991 from Mr 9 and has possessed and farmed it for 26 years with the authorities' written recognition; the defendants invaded in 2006 and were criminally convicted for it, then in 2016 forged a title certificate over her land and sold it to Mr C7. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 16][Para 17]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C1-2, with Ms C2 and Mr C7, represented by attorney Kea Sophal):** the family has lived on and occupied the land since 1986; Ms D came with some twenty soldiers and burned Mr C1's hut; the title was issued through the provincial land-dispute committee and the governor's decision, not by forgery, and the plaintiff never possessed the land, holding only a possession application that the commune chief himself disowned. [Para 26][Para 65][Para 68][Para 75]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** all of Ms D's claims are dismissed and she bears the costs; the right of appeal is reserved. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]
The court found the 1991 sale defective: no contract of sale of immovable property was ever drawn up, so the transfer did not comply with Article 41 of Decree-Law No. 38 on contracts or with Article 135 of the Civil Code, under which an agreement transferring immovable property has no effect unless registered — Mr 9 only signed an acknowledgment on 30 November 2006, fifteen years later. [Para 72][Para 73] The possession application of 10 February 1991 was itself irregular, bearing the village chief's signature before he took office, as he confirmed on 12 December 2007. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 74] Against that, the defendants' certificate followed the governor's decision of 11 December 2015 and the land department's procedure, so no forgery was made out; and the transfer from Mr C1 to Mr C7 was registered in accordance with Article 65 of the Land Law, which the court would not go behind. [Para 75][Para 78] Witnesses, including commune officials, placed Mr C1's family on the land long before, and mere possession applications could not outweigh registered ownership under Articles 134 and 135. [Para 76][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A sale of immovable property that is neither reduced to a contract nor registered transfers no ownership and cannot be set against a registered title; and a title certificate issued on a competent authority's decision, followed by a registered transfer, stands unless fraud is proved, an applicant's possession application and long de facto occupation being insufficient to displace it. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** that a document signed by a commune chief before he assumed office is irregular on its face, and that a court will not reopen the substance of an earlier criminal conviction for violence against a lawful possessor when the present claim is one of title. [Para 74][Para 19][Para 78]
