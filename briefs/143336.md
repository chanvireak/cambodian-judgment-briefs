# The plaintiff (name redacted) v Mr B and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 53 "K"/569, Banteay Meanchey Provincial Court, 31 July 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (president: Pok Sovann Mony) · first instance · Doc ID 143336

## 1. Facts
In 2013 Mr B (aged 43) and his wife Ms C2 borrowed 5,000 (five thousand) US dollars from the plaintiff (a woman of 57) at 250 US dollars of interest a month — 5% a month — recorded in a loan contract of 22 October 2013. [Para 7][Para 10][Para 12]
They paid interest for ten months, stopped, and after a complaint to the commune hall paid a further 3,000 US dollars, leaving 5,000 US dollars of principal outstanding; the plaintiff claimed that principal, 10,000,000 (ten million) riel per defendant for mental distress and lost time, provisional enforcement and costs. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether, the debt being admitted and no point of dispute raised, judgment follows for the 5,000 US dollars principal alone, the interest having been abandoned in the accounting? [Para 9][Para 14][Para 16]
2. Whether provisional enforcement lies over the damages head where the amount claimed is unliquidated, and whether 10,000,000 riel per defendant is recoverable for mental distress? [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (lender, name redacted):** she lent 5,000 US dollars in 2013 at 250 US dollars a month; the debtors paid interest for about a year and then stopped, and after the commune intervention they paid 3,000 US dollars against a total of 8,000 US dollars claimed, leaving 5,000 US dollars owing, for which she also seeks damages and immediate enforcement. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9]
**Defendants (Mr B and Ms C2, borrowers):** they admit borrowing the 5,000 US dollars at 250 US dollars a month, admit paying only ten months of interest before stopping, admit paying the further 3,000 US dollars, and admit that they still owe the principal. [Para 10][Para 11]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the debtors must pay the plaintiff 5,000 (five thousand) US dollars principal and 1,000,000 (one million) riel in compensation for mental distress; the rest of the claim is dismissed, the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to head 1 only, and costs lie on the debtors. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]
The court recorded that there was no disputed point at all. [Para 12] It found the plaintiff's account credible because the defendants had admitted the debt both in the preparatory proceedings and at the hearing, and it accepted that the loan contract of 22 October 2013 was formed by the parties' own will and admitted by the debtors, so that the right of claim existed. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] It therefore ordered repayment. [Para 17] On the 10,000,000 riel damages claim it held the head admissible under Article 398 of the Civil Code — the default had cost the plaintiff time and travel to court — but excessive relative to the principal, and awarded a sum proportionate to the actual loss. [Para 18] Provisional enforcement was allowed for the principal because the claim concerned rights over property, but refused for the damages head because the amount of that compensation was not yet fixed. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a debtor admits the loan and the outstanding principal, judgment enters for that principal even though the contractual interest was far above any lawful rate and has been dropped from the accounting; and provisional enforcement is refused for an unliquidated damages head precisely because its amount is not yet determined, while granted for the principal. [Para 16][Para 20][Para 25]

**Obiter dicta:** that damages under Article 398 of the Civil Code must bear a proportion to the principal sued upon, a claim of 10,000,000 riel per defendant being "excessive" against a 5,000 US dollar debt. [Para 18]
