# Ms D v Mr C (Civil Judgment No. 09 K, Preah Vihear Provincial Court, 7 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Chien Sros) · first instance · Doc ID 143344

## 1. Facts
Ms D lent the defendant money twice: 12,000,000 riel (twelve million riel) on 17 November 2014 and a further sum on 01 June 2015, both under written loan contracts. [Para 3] In 2016, before the commune chief, the parties signed an agreement acknowledging that the defendant owed 5,850 USD (five thousand eight hundred and fifty US dollars), repayable at 1,000 USD (one thousand US dollars) a month. [Para 4] He paid 1,000 USD once and then stopped; Ms D waived 50 USD of the outstanding 4,850 USD and sued for 4,800 USD, plus 2,000 USD moral damages and costs. [Para 6][Para 5] The defendant did not appear at the hearing of 22 October 2019. [Para 1]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a defendant who fails to appear at the oral-argument hearing is deemed to have confessed to the plaintiff's factual statements, so that the court must uphold the claim as pleaded? [Para 14]
2. Whether non-pecuniary damages lie for a debtor's failure to repay a loan under Article 400 of the Civil Code? [Para 17]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Ms D, creditor):** the loans are documented by written contracts and by the 2016 commune-level agreement in which the defendant acknowledged the 5,850 USD debt and promised 1,000 USD a month; he performed once only, and she claims the reduced balance of 4,800 USD, 2,000 USD for the time and expense of pursuing him from village to court, and costs on the losing debtor. [Para 8][Para 11][Para 12]
**Defendant (Mr C, debtor):** no submissions — he failed to appear at the oral-argument hearing, so no defence was advanced on the facts or on the amount. [Para 14]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed in part — the defendant is ordered to pay 4,800 USD (four thousand eight hundred US dollars) in principal to Ms D, the claim for 2,000 USD moral damages is dismissed, and costs are borne by the defendant. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]
The court applied Article 201 (new) paragraph 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure: where the defendant does not appear on the date fixed for oral argument, the court must treat him as having confessed to the plaintiff's statements of fact, and if it considers the claim well founded it enters judgment in his absence (សាលក្រមកំបាំងមុខ), otherwise it must dismiss. [Para 14] On that basis the court accepted that the defendant had received 4,800 USD from the plaintiff, and ordered repayment of exactly that sum. [Para 15][Para 20] On the moral-damages head it construed Article 400 (new) paragraph 2 of the Civil Code, which permits an award for mental suffering only where a personal interest has been infringed — honour, life or physical integrity — and held that a debt claim exhibits no such character, so that head was struck out. [Para 17][Para 18] Costs followed the defendant's loss. [Para 19]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A defendant who absents himself from the oral-argument hearing is deemed to have admitted the plaintiff's factual allegations, and the court may enter judgment against him in absentia upon the claim as pleaded — but default of appearance establishes only the facts, not the legal character of the claim, so a head of damage that the law does not allow on those facts must still be dismissed. [Para 14][Para 18]

**Obiter dicta:** the rule of Article 201 cuts both ways — the same provision obliges the court to dismiss the claim where it considers it unfounded, so the deemed confession does not guarantee success. [Para 14] The court also noted that costs fall on the party who loses. [Para 19]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The second loan is stated as 18,000,000 riel (eighteen million riel) in the plaintiff's narrative but as 17,830,000 riel (seventeen million eight hundred and thirty thousand riel) in the summary of undisputed facts; both figures are reported as printed. [Para 3][Para 4] The 2016 agreement is dated 06 June 2016 in the list of evidence while repayments are said to begin on 10 June 2016. [Para 3][Para 13] The defendant's name survives only as a bare digit in the party heading and is transcribed inconsistently elsewhere in the OCR; it is rendered here as Mr C and is not a real name. [Para 2] The standard disclaimer "this is a factual judgment and not a model judgment" is spliced mid-sentence into paragraphs 3, 8 and 25 by the OCR. [Para 25]
