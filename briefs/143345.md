# Microfinance institution (name redacted) v Mr C1 and Ms C2 (Civil Judgment No. 017 "ច" (Ch), Takéo Provincial Court, 5 December 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Phin Phaleng) · first instance · Doc ID 143345

## 1. Facts
On 07 May 2015 the plaintiff microfinance institution lent the defendant spouses 20,000 USD (twenty thousand US dollars) over 60 months at 1.4% per month, principal repayable every four months and interest monthly per the schedule of 11 May 2015, with a 3% monthly penalty on any principal outstanding after final maturity on 11 May 2020. [Para 9] The loan was secured by mortgages over the defendants' residential plot and a rice field. [Para 13][Para 18] The defendants paid 12,195.98 USD in total — 6,670 USD of principal and 5,525.98 USD of interest — leaving 13,330 USD (thirteen thousand three hundred and thirty US dollars) of principal, which they admit owing. [Para 10][Para 13][Para 20]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether the defendants must pay contractual interest of 1.4% per month on 13,330 USD from 11 May 2017 until payment, given their plea of incapacity to pay? [Para 14][Para 22]
2. Whether the 3% penalty for late payment after 11 May 2020, and 12,000,000 riel of lost-time damages, are payable now? [Para 14][Para 26]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (microfinance institution, creditor):** the loan contract and mortgage deeds establish the 20,000 USD advance and the arrears; the defendants admit the balance but have stopped paying, so it claims the 13,330 USD principal, 1.4% monthly interest from 11 May 2017, the 3% penalty after final maturity, 12,000,000 riel for years of lost time, and provisional enforcement of the principal. [Para 12][Para 15][Para 33]
**Defendants (Mr C1 and Ms C2, debtors):** they acknowledge the debt and ask to repay 200,000 riel a month from September 2019, promising to clear the balance by selling two hectares of rice field, and ask the court to waive interest, penalty and damages because they have no means to pay. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 29]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed — the defendants must pay 13,330 USD (thirteen thousand three hundred and thirty US dollars) in principal, interest at 1.4 (one point four) per cent per month on that sum from 11 May 2017 until paid, and 12,000,000 riel (twelve million riel) for lost time; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal, and costs fall on the defendants. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]
The court first fixed liability: the loan contract, the repayment schedule and the two mortgage deeds proved the 20,000 USD advance, the payments made and the admitted balance of 13,330 USD, so it gave effect to the parties' agreement on the principal. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21] On interest it held that inability to pay is no defence where the debtor signed the contract of his own accord, Article 398 (new) paragraph 1 of the Civil Code being the governing provision, and it then tested the rate against the National Bank of Cambodia's Prakas No. B7.017.109 Prokor of 13 March 2017, Article 4, which caps credit interest at 18% a year: 1.4% a month is within the ceiling, so the rate was enforced. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] It refused the 3% penalty, because final maturity on 11 May 2020 had not yet arrived, making the claim one for a future period that would presume a future default the court could not assume. [Para 26][Para 27] It allowed the lost-time damages, finding the defendants at fault and their breach seriously prejudicial, again under Article 398 (1), and noting the total claim did not exceed 27% of the sums demanded. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] Provisional enforcement followed from the admission of the principal, and costs from the defendants' fault under Article 64 (new) paragraph 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contractual rate of interest is enforceable against a defaulting borrower where it stays within the National Bank of Cambodia's 18% annual ceiling, incapacity to pay being no answer to a contract freely signed; but a penalty stipulated to accrue only after a future maturity date is not yet due, and a court will not award it on the assumption that the debtor will default in the future. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 27]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a total claim of principal, interest and damages amounting to no more than 27% of the sums demanded raises no question of excess. [Para 32] The further observation that the judgment is provisionally enforceable because the principal is admitted. [Para 34]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The defendants are identified only as C1 and C2 and the creditor institution by a single initial; these are OCR redactions, not names. [Para 1][Para 3] The signature block names the court as "Battambang" although this is a Takéo judgment, and the trial judge's name is rendered variously as Phin Phaleng and Phin Phallong. [Para 42] The lost-time figure appears once as "120000000 million riel" (12,000,000 million) at [Para 29] but as 12,000,000 riel in the claim and the disposition. [Para 7][Para 38] The standard disclaimer that this is a factual and not a model judgment is spliced into the middle of sentences at [Para 9] and [Para 23].
