# Bank D v Mr 01, Ms 02 and Ms 03 (Civil Judgment No. 57 K, Kandal Provincial Court, 7 November 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (Kim Rath Narin) · first instance · Doc ID 143347

## 1. Facts
On 30 March 2018 the three co-defendants signed a loan contract with the plaintiff bank for 35,000 USD (thirty-five thousand US dollars) at 12% a year over 72 months, repaying 688 USD a month from 09 May 2018 to 09 March 2024 with the balance due on 30 March 2024, secured by a mortgage over 124.16 m² of immovable property. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 40] They stopped paying on 09 July 2018 and did not resume despite repeated written and oral reminders. [Para 15][Para 17] The outstanding principal is 34,450.05 USD, which they admit. [Para 21][Para 41]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether co-defendants who were duly summoned but absented themselves from the oral-argument hearing are deemed to have confessed, so that judgment may be entered against them in their absence under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure? [Para 33]
2. Whether penalty interest fixed at twice the contractual rate is enforceable, and what sum the 5,000 USD claim for legal service, lost time and moral damage should yield? [Para 43][Para 45]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Bank D, creditor):** the loan and mortgage contracts of 30 March 2018 prove the advance; the co-defendants defaulted in July 2018 and ignored every reminder, so it claims 34,450.05 USD of principal, 12% annual interest, penalty interest at twice the contract rate, 5,000 USD for legal service, lost time and mental suffering, costs, and provisional enforcement of the first three heads. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 12][Para 17]
**Defendants (Mr 01, Ms 02 and Ms 03, debtors):** before the hearing they admitted borrowing 35,000 USD at 12%, admitted owing 34,450.05 USD and accepted the 12% rate, but pleaded inability to pay, asked the bank to waive the penalty, legal fees, lost time and the 5,000 USD, objected to provisional enforcement, and offered 400 USD a month — then failed to appear on 31 October 2019. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 33]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed — the co-defendants must jointly pay 34,450.05 USD (thirty-four thousand four hundred and fifty US dollars and five cents) in principal, interest at 12% a year from 09 July 2018 until payment, penalty interest at twice the contract rate from the same date, and 10,000,000 riel (ten million riel) for legal service, lost time and moral damage; the judgment is provisionally enforceable as to the principal, interest and penalty, and costs fall on the co-defendants. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
The court found the pleading regular and its own jurisdiction established under Article 7 of the Code of Civil Procedure, and took the loan contract and the mortgage contract of 30 March 2018 — over a 124.16 m² plot under a land-use request of 22 April 2011 — as proving the 35,000 USD advance at 12% over 72 months. [Para 29][Para 40] Because the co-defendants had been summoned for 31 October 2019 and stayed away without excuse, the court held them deemed to have confessed to the plaintiff's statements of fact and proceeded by default judgment under Article 201. [Para 33] On that footing it accepted the admitted balance of 34,450.05 USD and the fact of default. [Para 41] It upheld the 12% rate as consistent with Article 585 of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 108 Ka.Y/Prak/11 of 21 December 2011 of the Ministry of Justice, [Para 42] and upheld penalty interest at double the contractual rate because clause 2.2 of the loan contract expressly provided for it on late payment. [Para 44] The 5,000 USD claim for legal service, lost time and mental suffering was not taken at face value; the court assessed it "according to what is appropriate in fact" and awarded 10,000,000 riel instead. [Para 45][Para 49] Provisional enforcement was granted on the admitted principal, interest and penalty under Article 196, and costs were placed on the co-defendants under Article 64. [Para 46][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a loan contract stipulates that late payment attracts penalty interest at twice the contractual rate, the court enforces that stipulation as the parties' agreement, provided the contract rate itself is lawful; and co-defendants duly summoned who absent themselves without excuse are deemed to have confessed the plaintiff's factual allegations, so the court may enter judgment against them in their absence on the claim as pleaded. [Para 33][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the court's territorial jurisdiction followed from Article 7 of the Code of Civil Procedure and that provisional enforcement was available under Article 196 because the debt was admitted and evidenced. [Para 29][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The debtors appear only as numbers (01, 02, 03) and one of them has no name at all in the OCR; the creditor is "Bank D" but is called "Bank A" once in the reasoning. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 30] The instalment schedule is said to run to 09 March 2014 in the narrative but to 2024 elsewhere, and the final payment is separately given as 30 March 2024. [Para 13][Para 16] The claim for legal service, lost time and moral damage is 5,000 USD in the pleading but 10,000,000 riel in the disposition, with no conversion stated. [Para 11][Para 45][Para 49] Paragraphs 27 and 47 survive only as fragments ("ភាព ។", "ចម្លើយ ។"), the contract and land-title numbers are blank, and the closing representation clauses of the disposition are badly garbled. [Para 27][Para 47][Para 48] The recital that the judgment is factual and not a model judgment is spliced mid-sentence at several points. [Para 33]
