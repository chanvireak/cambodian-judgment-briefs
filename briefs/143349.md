# Mr D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 01 Ch, Takéo Provincial Court, 23 January 2020)
Bench: 1 judge (Kong Phumint) · first instance · Doc ID 143349

## 1. Facts
Over four transactions between 31 December 2012 and 14 October 2014 Ms C stood personal guarantor for three loans made by Mr D to third parties (1,500,000; 2,000,000; and 2,000,000 riel) and herself borrowed 1,000,000 riel, each transaction recorded in a written loan and personal-guarantee contract at 3% (three per cent) interest a month, all made known to the village and commune authorities. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 14] The principal totals 6,500,000 riel (six million five hundred thousand riel); she paid interest until October 2014 and then stopped altogether. [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19] She was summoned but did not appear at either the preparatory or the oral-argument hearing, without valid reason. [Para 26][Para 36]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a stipulated interest rate of 3% a month may be enforced as agreed, or must be reduced to the statutory ceiling? [Para 32][Para 33]
2. Whether a guarantor who is also a borrower, and who absents herself without excuse, is liable for the whole 6,500,000 riel and for the 4,000,000 riel claimed as legal costs and lost time? [Para 31][Para 34]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (Mr D, lender):** the four written contracts, all known to the commune authorities, show that Ms C guaranteed three loans and took one herself; she paid interest only until October 2014 and then avoided him, so he claims 6,500,000 riel of principal, 3% monthly interest running from 31 December 2012, 4,000,000 riel for legal service and lost time, and costs. [Para 10][Para 13][Para 19][Para 21]
**Defendant (Ms C, guarantor and borrower):** no case was advanced — she was duly summoned and stayed away from both hearings without valid reason, so the court proceeded under Article 201 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 26][Para 36]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim is allowed in substance — Ms C must pay 6,500,000 riel (six million five hundred thousand riel) in principal, interest at 18% a year on that sum from 31 December 2012 until full payment, and 500,000 riel (five hundred thousand riel) for legal service and lost time; the excess of that last claim is dismissed, and costs fall on the defendant. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]
The court first found the statement of claim regular under Articles 74 and 75 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 28] On the four contracts and the plaintiff's statements it held it proved that Ms C had both borrowed and stood guarantor, and that she still owed the 6,500,000 riel principal, a point recorded as undisputed. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 27] On interest it refused the contractual 3% a month as exceeding the lawful rate, and applied Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code together with Article 1 of Prakas No. 128 Kay.Prak/11 of 21 December 2011, fixing the award at 18% a year from 31 December 2012. [Para 33][Para 38] The 4,000,000 riel claim for legal service and lost time was found both heavy for the defendant and unsupported by evidence, and was trimmed to 500,000 riel as appropriate to the actual situation, the balance being dismissed. [Para 34][Para 39] Costs followed under Article 64. [Para 35][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contractual rate of 3% a month — 36% a year — exceeds the lawful ceiling and is replaced by the court with interest at 18% a year under Article 17 of the Law on Implementation of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 128; and a claim for legal costs and lost time that is disproportionate and unsupported by evidence is allowed only at a modest sum appropriate to the actual situation. [Para 33][Para 34]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a guarantor who has signed personal-guarantee contracts before the commune authorities is bound by them, and that absence without valid reason after due summons permits judgment under Article 201. [Para 14][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The disposition fixes interest at 18% a year but gives the parenthetical words as "fifteen per cent" (ដប់ បាំ បី ភាគរយ); both are reported as printed. [Para 38] In the narrative the first borrower appears as "09" and elsewhere as "1", and the other two borrowers survive only as bare digits. [Para 6][Para 10] The parties are reduced to the initials D and C throughout, with the plaintiff's parents and wife also recorded as "father D", "mother D" and "wife D". [Para 1] The clerk's name appears as both Tang Mithy and Tarng Butthy, and the paragraphs setting out the claim and the reasoning are badly fragmented by the OCR. [Para 43][Para 16][Para 17]
