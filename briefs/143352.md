# Ms D v Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 215 K, Supreme Court, 2 July 2020)
Bench: 5 judges (president: Chiv Chea) · First cassation, points of law only · Doc ID 143352

## 1. Facts
In 2017 Ms C asked Ms D, who had no money of her own, to stand as guarantor on a 17,000,000 riel (seventeen million riel) loan from a private lender, repayable over twelve months at 3% per month — 510,000 riel a month — secured by a 1,047 m² plot and recorded in a written contract. [Para 10] Interest was paid for the opening months only; as guarantor Ms D then kept paying the lender monthly, and Ms C refused to repay principal or interest to either the lender or the guarantor. [Para 11]
Prey Veng Provincial Court (12 September 2018) ordered Ms C to pay the 17,000,000 riel principal with 18% annual interest from 6 September 2017, plus 2,000,000 riel lost-time damages, costs on the defendant. [Para 12] The Phnom Penh Appeal Court (8 July 2019) annulled that judgment and dismissed Ms D's claim outright. [Para 21]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether an appellate court may dismiss a claim for want of evidence without examining the contract admitted by the defendant and the creditor's statement already on the file? [Para 38]
2. Whether the Supreme Court may adjudicate when the cassation appellant does not appear at the hearing? [Para 34]

## 3. Arguments
**Appellant (Ms D, plaintiff and guarantor):** she lent nothing of her own; she guaranteed Ms C's borrowing, was pursued by the creditor as guarantor, repaid it in full and now recovers what she paid — and she also contests the 18% annual rate substituted by the first-instance court. [Para 31]
**Respondent (Ms C, defendant):** she signed and thumbprinted the documents but never received the money; the house plan she pledged belonged to her brother, and she chased the creditor repeatedly without being paid. [Para 25]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the cassation appeal is accepted, the Appeal Court judgment of 8 July 2019 is set aside, and the case is remitted to the Phnom Penh Appeal Court for a fresh hearing; Supreme Court costs are borne by the respondent. [Para 45]
The court first held the appeal admissible and in time, and that on a first cassation it reviews points of law only under Articles 284–285 of the Code of Civil Procedure; the appellant's absence did not prevent adjudication because her written grounds and her authorised representative were before the bench. [Para 33][Para 35] On the merits it found the Appeal Court's reasoning insufficient. Dismissing a claim for absence of evidence presupposes that the evidence on file was actually examined, and that court had considered neither the loan contract — which is the law of the parties and which the defendant admits she duly thumbprinted — nor the creditor's statement taken in the proceedings, nor whether anything showed the defendant never received the money when she signed. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] Those documents were never weighed, so the dismissal rested on no sufficient basis and the case had to return to the Appeal Court. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An appellate court that dismisses a claim for lack of evidence errs in law, reviewably on cassation, where it failed to examine the specific documents in the file — a contract admitted by the party against whom it is used, and a creditor's statement — that bear directly on the disputed fact. [Para 38][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a first cassation is confined to points of law under Articles 284–285, and that a party's absence does not bar adjudication where written submissions and a representative are present. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The contract is dated 06 May 2017 in the facts but 06 June 2017 in the reasoning. [Para 10][Para 38] Interest is described as paid for three months in the facts and for two months in the party statements. [Para 11][Para 30] Party names survive only as initials in the OCR and the creditor appears as a bare digit; these are reported as OCR losses, not as names.
