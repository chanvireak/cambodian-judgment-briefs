# Ms D v Mr C and Ms C (Civil Judgment No. 165 "K1", Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 10 February 2020)
Bench: 1 judge (Than Leng) · first instance · Doc ID 143353

## 1. Facts
On 19 September 2018 Mr C and his wife Ms C borrowed 97,500 USD (ninety-seven thousand five hundred dollars) from Ms D to on-lend at interest to factory workers, signing an agreement of that date and pledging two plots in Kampot province (certificates of 05 June 2014); Ms D paid the money over at once. [Para 14] They promised to repay 20,000 USD on 04 January 2019 per a money-delivery letter, with the balance in February 2019, then borrowed a further sum against two Phnom Penh plots (certificates of 11 July 2017), bringing the total to 128,000 USD (one hundred and twenty-eight thousand dollars) — and paid neither principal nor interest. [Para 15][Para 16]
Sued for the principal, 18% annual interest, confiscation of the four plots and 20,000 USD in damages and legal fees, the defendants were served but did not appear and filed no defence. [Para 5][Para 10][Para 11][Para 21]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a borrower who has received the full principal and paid nothing is liable for that principal with statutory interest at 18% per year? [Para 32][Para 34]
2. Whether the court can order the confiscation (រឹបអូស) of immovable property pledged as security in the same judgment that establishes the debt? [Para 36]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (lender, Ms D):** she advanced the money in cash on the strength of the written agreement and the pledged title certificates because she trusted people she had long known; the defendants promised 20,000 USD by 04 January 2019 and the rest in February 2019, then stalled from day to day and eventually absconded, so she claims the whole 128,000 USD with 18% annual interest, the four pledged plots, and 20,000 USD for lost time and legal services. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 34]
**Defendants (borrowers, Mr C and Ms C):** they entered no appearance and advanced no contentions; the record shows only that they were summonsed in accordance with procedure and failed to attend without excuse, so no fact was contested and the court proceeded on the documents. [Para 21][Para 31][Para 38]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** the defendants are ordered jointly to pay 128,000 USD (one hundred and twenty-eight thousand dollars) with interest at 18% per year on that sum from February 2019 until full payment, plus 20,000,000 riel (twenty million riel) in damages for lost time and legal services; all further claims are dismissed and costs fall on the defendants. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]
On the documents — the loan contract of 09 March 2018, the agreement of 19 September 2018 and the delivery-and-receipt letter of 01 January 2019 — the court found the 128,000 USD borrowing proved and no repayment ever made. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 38] The 18% rate was held consistent with Articles 584 and 585 of the Civil Code and with Prakas No. 128 on capped interest rates of 21 December 2011 of the Ministry of Justice, so the claim was allowed as pleaded. [Para 34] Non-payment of instalments of principal and interest was characterised as breach of the parties' contractual duty, grounding principal, penalty interest and costs. [Para 35] The request to confiscate the four plots was refused as premature: confiscation of immovable property may be carried out only in compulsory execution proceedings, not in the substantive judgment. [Para 36] Damages were allowed "as appropriate" on the claim as pleaded. [Para 37] Provisional enforcement was ordered under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure and costs taxed under Article 64. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A pledge of immovable property secures the debt but does not entitle the creditor to an order confiscating the pledged property in the judgment on the debt: realisation of the security belongs exclusively to compulsory execution proceedings, and a claim for it in the substantive action is refused as premature. [Para 36]

**Obiter dicta:** the acceptance of an 18% annual rate as consistent with Articles 584–585 of the Civil Code and Prakas No. 128 of 21 December 2011, and the observation that provisional enforcement is available under Article 196 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 34][Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The damages claim is pleaded at 20,000 USD but the award is 20,000,000 riel, roughly a quarter of the sum claimed; both are recorded as printed. [Para 11][Para 37][Para 41] The first loan is dated 09 March 2018 in the contract and in the list of evidence, while the parties' narrative places the borrowing on 19 September 2018, and interest is made to run from February 2019 although default is said to begin on 04 January 2019. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 22][Para 41] In the plaintiff's statement the wife is transcribed as "C1" instead of "C2", and the operative order repeats "ឈ្មោះ ឈ្មោះ". [Para 17][Para 41] The watermark sentence "this judgment is a real case, not a model judgment" is interleaved at paras 9, 17, 26, 38 and 46, breaking the sentences around it. [Para 9][Para 26][Para 38] Party names survive only as initials and title-certificate numbers are lost; none has been expanded.
