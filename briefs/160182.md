# X v Y (Model judgment, Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, 20 February 2015)
Bench: 1 judge (name redacted, "J") · specimen, not decided · Doc ID 160182

## 1. Facts
On 01 November 2007 X lent Y 10,000 USD (ten thousand dollars) under a written loan contract repayable on 31 October 2008, with interest at 5% a year and a further 10% a year as damages for delay in performance. [Para 18][Para 22]
On 31 October 2012 Y paid X 1,000 USD and X gave a receipt stating that the sum was part-performance of that loan contract; Y later denied this and said the 1,000 discharged a different obligation. [Para 24][Para 30][Para 34] On 31 October 2013 X bought a car from Y for 7,000 USD and took delivery; X said the price was paid in December 2013, Y denied receiving it. [Para 25][Para 35][Para 37]
X sued for 13,500 USD — the 10,000 principal with 3,500 in damages for delay plus 10% a year on the principal from 01 November 2012 — with costs and provisional enforcement. [Para 12][Para 14][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether a part-payment of 1,000 USD, acknowledged in a receipt as performance of the loan, was performance of that loan so as to suspend the prescription of the debt? [Para 29][Para 38][Para 39]
2. Whether the borrower's claim to set off the 7,000 USD car price succeeds where the lender asserts, without evidence, that she paid that price? [Para 35][Para 40]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (X, lender):** the 1,000 USD received on 31 October 2012 was accepted and receipted as part-payment of the loan itself, and she paid the 7,000 USD car price in December 2013, so nothing is owed to Y on the car and the full balance of the loan with contractual interest and delay damages is due. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 35]
**Defendant (Y, borrower):** the 1,000 USD was payment of an unrelated obligation and not of this loan, the loan claim is time-barred, and the 7,000 USD car price remains unpaid by X and must be set off against whatever is owed; he had repeatedly demanded that price without result. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Y is ordered to pay 7,500 USD (seven thousand five hundred dollars) with 10% a year on that sum from 01 November 2013 until full payment; all further claims are dismissed; costs are taxed 75% against Y and 25% against X; points 1 and 3 are provisionally enforceable. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]
On the first issue the court preferred the written receipt (evidence X3) over Y's denial and found the 1,000 USD to have been part-performance of the loan, so the running of prescription was suspended. [Para 38][Para 39] On the second it found no evidence at all that X had paid the 7,000 USD car price, rejected her rebuttal, and treated the price as a debt of X to Y available for set-off. [Para 40][Para 41] It then struck the account: at 31 October 2012 the debt stood at 10,000 principal, 500 interest and 4,000 delay damages; the 1,000 paid that day was appropriated first to the 500 interest and then to 500 of the damages, leaving 10,000 principal and 3,500 damages. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Carrying that to 31 October 2013 gives 10,000 principal and 4,500 damages, from which the 7,000 car price is deducted under Articles 472 and 451 of the Civil Code, leaving 7,500 USD. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52] Because X succeeded only in part, costs were apportioned rather than charged wholly to the loser. [Para 50][Para 53]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A payment of part of a debt, acknowledged in writing by the creditor as performance of that debt, is performance for prescription purposes and interrupts the running of the limitation period against the whole balance; and where payments are appropriated, interest and delay damages are discharged before principal, under Articles 472 and 451 of the Civil Code. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 44][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** the preliminary observations on the form of judgments — that a judgment and an order (ដីកាសម្រេច) are appealed differently, by appeal and by objection respectively, and that a judgment fixes the legal relations as they stood at the close of oral argument. [Para 3][Para 6][Para 7]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is a specimen judgment overlaid with drafting commentary: paragraphs 1–9 and the notes at paras 12–13, 18–20, 31–32 and 51–52 are instructions to the drafter, not findings, and the parties appear only as placeholders "X" and "Y" with counsel as "P" and "B". [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 18][Para 31] The case number and judgment number are left blank, and Articles 187–188 are attributed to the Civil Code though they are articles of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 1][Para 8][Para 9] The claim runs interest from 01 November 2012 but the award runs it from 01 November 2013, the date of the set-off, which the judgment does not expressly reconcile. [Para 14][Para 55] No party name or gender survives, and none has been supplied.
