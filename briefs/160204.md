# X v Y (Model judgment, Ratanakiri Provincial Court, 28 June 2019)
Bench: 1 judge (name redacted, "J") · specimen, not decided · Doc ID 160204

## 1. Facts
On 30 November 2018 X sold "land L" to Y for 100,000 USD (one hundred thousand dollars) by private written sale instrument; 50,000 USD was payable when the application to register the whole transfer was lodged and the remaining 50,000 USD within one month of lodging it, that is by 31 December 2018. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 27] Y paid the first 50,000 and X delivered the land on 31 December 2018. [Para 19][Para 30]
Y did not pay the balance. On 11 January 2019 X demanded it within two weeks, warning that rescission would follow, and on 25 January 2019, nothing having been paid, X declared the sale rescinded and sued for cancellation of the registered transfer. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 14]
Y's defence was that on 31 December 2018, when X asked about delivery, he had messaged "please wait three more months" and X had answered "I understand — next year will be better", which he says varied the payment date to 31 March 2019. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44]

## 2. Legal issues
1. Whether that exchange of messages concluded a binding agreement postponing the balance of the price to 31 March 2019? [Para 38][Para 44][Para 68]
2. Whether the seller's declaration of rescission was effective for serious breach, and what each party must restore on rescission? [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72]

## 3. Arguments
**Plaintiff (X, seller):** no agreement to postpone was ever formed — the message he received asked for time and his reply merely acknowledged the reason Y could not pay on time, without accepting a new date; Y remained in default after a two-week demand, the breach was serious, and the rescission declared on 25 January 2019 therefore took effect, so the registered transfer must be cancelled. [Para 45][Para 50][Para 51][Para 69]
**Defendant (Y, buyer):** X created the varied agreement of 31 December 2018 by answering "I understand — next year will be better", and he invoked his right to withhold counter-performance, refusing cancellation of the registration unless he first recovers the 50,000 USD he had paid together with 5% a year from 30 November 2018 — a right he expressly reserved in his answer of 01 April 2019. [Para 31][Para 44][Para 48][Para 71][Para 72]

## 4. Judgment and reasoning
**Outcome:** Y is ordered to carry out the registration of the immovable recorded in the appendix in X's favour, in exchange for X's repayment of 50,000 USD (fifty thousand dollars) with 5% a year on that sum from 30 November 2018 until paid; the rest of X's claim is dismissed; costs are divided into three parts, one borne by X and two by Y. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]
The court first fixed the admitted facts: the contract, its two-stage price, the delivery of the land, the missed deadline, the demand and the declaration of rescission. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 56] It then rejected the alleged variation. Although the messages and the telephone conversation were admitted, there was no evidence precise enough to show that X had done any act accepting Y's proposal; the words relied on were not a clear acceptance, and an "assertion" (ការអះអាង) is not the same thing as a "statement" (ការថ្លែងការ) taken in examination. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 62][Para 67][Para 68] The contractual date therefore stood, Y was in default from 31 December 2018 and failed even the two-week demand, which the court characterised as serious breach under Article 408 of the Civil Code. [Para 69] X's conditional declaration of rescission was accordingly effective under Articles 409 and 325, and the sale was rescinded. [Para 70] On rescission Y must undo the registered transfer under Article 411, while X must return the 50,000 USD received with 5% annual interest from 30 November 2018 under Articles 411 and 318 — the two obligations being concurrent. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] Because X's claim succeeded only in that limited, reciprocal form, costs were apportioned rather than charged wholly to Y. [Para 74][Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party who asks for more time and receives only an acknowledgment of his reason — "I understand" — has not obtained a variation of the contractual payment date; the original date stands, and non-payment after a reasonable demand is serious breach entitling the seller to rescind. On rescission the restoration obligations of seller and buyer are concurrent: the buyer must undo the registered transfer and the seller must refund the price received with interest from the date of payment. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the distinction drawn between a party's "assertion" under Article 95 of the Code of Civil Procedure and his "statement" on personal examination, and the remark that a court must assess the conduct of the oral hearing and not merely the results of the evidence. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 61]
